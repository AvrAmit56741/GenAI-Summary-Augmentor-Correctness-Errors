\paragraph{Claims synthesized from several sentences.} Summary sentences often fuse information from several parts of the article (Figure~\ref{fig:multisource}). In 39 Injected reference-summary sentences annotated with their evidence, 59\% need two or more article sentences; an automatic rule (no single article sentence covers half of the claim's prefix-stripped content words; 77\% agreement with the annotation) puts the share at 56\% of 1,472 sentences, ranging from 26\% to 73\% across sources. We score single sentences against the full article, using 196 single-source and 140 multi-source (reference, corrupted) sentence pairs from a stratified sample. SummaC-ZS, which relies on the best-matching individual article sentence, breaks on multi-source claims: at a threshold that catches 80\% of corrupted sentences, it flags 81\% [73, 86] of the \emph{faithful} multi-source sentences versus 43\% [36, 50] of single-source ones, and it scores the reference above its corrupted version only 59\% vs.\ 73\% of the time. Both gaps hold within every claim-length tercile. MorphEvalHeb, which reads the whole article, is unaffected: its false alarms are 4\% vs.\ 4\%, and its paired detection is 87\% vs.\ 92\% (difference not significant). The failure already appears when locating evidence. For 52 corrupted claims whose source sentences we annotated, an NLI model ranks a true source sentence first for only 35\% of them, versus 67\% for their faithful versions: a contradicted claim has no entailing sentence to find. A reranker combined with morphology-aware word overlap still finds it for 92\% (98\% for faithful claims). Restricting verification to retrieved evidence, as chunk- and claim-level checkers do \citep{zha-etal-2023-alignscore,tang-etal-2024-minicheck}, reintroduces the problem: MorphEvalHeb applied to the top three retrieved sentences raises false alarms on multi-source claims to 42\%, against 6\% for single-source claims and 4\% with the full article.
