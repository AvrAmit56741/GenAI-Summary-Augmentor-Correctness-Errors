# Metric papers validate on yesterday's generators

Papers that propose a **new evaluation metric** usually validate it on outputs from models **2–4 years older than the paper**. In multilingual summarization the gap reaches **5.5–7 years**: STEMF (EACL Findings 2026) is still validated on 2020 mT5 summaries and on machine-translated FRANK outputs from 2017–2019 systems. Papers that propose a **new benchmark** have, since 2024, usually used generators **0–1 years old**, often including the closed frontier model of their year (GPT-5 in VeriGray, Gemini-2.5-Pro and Claude-4 in WMT25 and CodeJudgeBench). Generators stay current only where an institution refreshes them: WMT adds frontier LLMs every year, long-form factuality benchmarks regenerate their responses, and saturated reward-model benchmarks get replaced by successors. Everywhere else, frontier closed models mostly appear as **judges** scoring much older **generator** outputs. The evidence that a metric's quality depends on the generator's era or strength is strongest in English summarization (Tang et al. 2023: about 10 balanced-accuracy points lost going from older to then-current fine-tuned summarizers on CNN/DM; also UniSumEval, Ramprasad 2024 and Zhong & Litman 2025). There is supporting but caveated evidence for code and for judges (CodeJudgeBench, JudgeBench, and Shelf-Life at ICLR 2026, which covers reasoning tasks only, not summarization). In MT the picture is mixed: WMT24 found that fine-tuned metrics do not break on LLM translations. **No multilingual paper was found that holds one metric fixed and compares old against new generators.** For MorphEvalHeb, both headline test sets are either old (Natural: about 2021–22-era generators, machine-translated, so a gap of about 5–6 years at ARR January 2027) or synthetic (Injected: 2022 human references with LLM-injected errors). That is the posture reviewers have criticized since Tang 2023. The current-model summaries the project already has (Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5.6-luna, Qwen3-32B, GLM-4.7-flash, Ministral-3-14B, DictaLM 3) are the cheapest way out, but only once they carry human labels.

## Metric papers trail benchmark papers by two to six years

The verified picture across task families splits papers into two groups. *Metric* papers reuse static human-labelled sets, so their age gap grows every year the set is reused. *Benchmark* papers generate fresh outputs, so their gap stays near zero. The table below applies the verification notes over the first-pass surveys. "Age gap" is the paper year minus the release year of the newest model whose **outputs** were evaluated; judge models are excluded.

Across this table, **closed frontier models appear far more often as judges than as generators.** FineSurE uses GPT-4-turbo to judge summaries from 2017–2020 systems ([arXiv 2407.00908](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00908)). Atasoy et al. (2026) use GPT-5-mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash as judges on QAGS-C and SummEval, which are 2017–2020 outputs, a gap of 6 years ([arXiv 2605.08462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08462)). STEMF trains 2024 Qwen2.5 and Gemma-2 evaluators and tests them on 2017–2020 summaries ([arXiv 2507.20752](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20752)). In code, CodeJudge-Eval's 12 judges include GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet, which are newer than its newest generator, Claude-3-Opus ([arXiv 2408.10718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10718)). ProcessBench evaluates o1-mini as a critic (F1 87.9) of steps written only by open Qwen and Llama models ([arXiv 2412.06559](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06559)). Among 33 multilingual LLM-as-judge papers, **GPT-family judges appear in 26 (79%)** and **10 (30%) use GPT as their only judge** ([Doğruöz et al., arXiv 2607.02235](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02235)). The reverse also occurs, a judge *older or weaker* than the generator. Dicta-LM 3.0 judges Hebrew summaries with GPT-4o against Gemini-2.5-Pro reference summaries ([arXiv 2602.02104](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02104)). CodeUltraFeedback uses GPT-3.5-Turbo to judge GPT-4-Turbo code ([arXiv 2403.09032](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09032v3)). WMT25 is the case where the two roles coincide: its LLM judges (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4.1, Claude-4, DeepSeek-V3, Llama-4-Maverick) are the same models as its translators ([WMT25 eval findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.24.pdf)).

## English summarization benchmarks reached GPT-5 while English metrics stayed on BART

English summarization shows the split between metric and benchmark papers most clearly. Every metric paper from SummaC through AMRFact, FIZZ and FineSurE meta-evaluates on SummaC-bench, TRUE, FRANK, SummEval or AggreFact-FtSota. The newest generators in those sets are BART, PEGASUS and T5, at 0.6B parameters or less. FRANK itself contains only 2017–2019 systems: LSTM S2S, PGN, BottomUp, BertSum and BART on CNN/DM, and TConvS2S, PGN, TranS2S and BertS2S on XSum ([ar5iv 2104.13346](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.13346)). FineSurE (ACL 2024) has human labels only on FRANK and REALSumm. Its "8 LLMs" summaries, from Gemini-1-pro, GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4-turbo and GPT-4-omni plus four open models, are **scored only by FineSurE, with no human faithfulness labels** ([arXiv 2407.00908](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00908)). MiniCheck's summarization subsets are AggreFact-FtSota (BART, PEGASUS, T5) and TofuEval's five LLMs, with **no GPT-4 summaries** ([arXiv 2404.10774](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10774)).

Benchmark papers moved much faster. Zhang et al. (TACL 2024) evaluated GPT-3, InstructGPT davinci v2, OPT 175B, GLM 130B, Cohere xlarge and Anthropic-LM v4-s3, all from 2022, a gap of 2 ([arXiv 2301.13848](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13848)). TofuEval (NAACL 2024) used **five** LLMs: GPT-3.5-Turbo, Vicuna-7B and WizardLM-7B/13B/30B ([arXiv 2402.13249](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13249)). UniSumEval (EMNLP Findings 2024) spans BART and Flan-T5, Phi-2, Llama2-13B-Chat, Mistral-7B, Mixtral and GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4-turbo and Claude 2.1 ([arXiv 2409.19898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.19898)). FaithBench (NAACL 2025 short) has 660 summaries, 66 passages from each of 10 LLMs across 8 families, including GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet. The authors chose "the smallest version in its latest generation" of each family ([arXiv 2410.13210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13210)). MSumBench (ACL 2025) covers BART and mT5, three open models of 27–72B, and GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet and Gemini-1.5-pro ([arXiv 2506.00549](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00549)). VeriGray, which is arXiv-only at v4, adds GPT-5, DeepSeek-V3-0324 and Qwen3-8B to FaithBench's documents ([arXiv 2510.21118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21118)). OmniCSEval (2026, venue unconfirmed) covers 28 LLMs including GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro, but validates its judge on only **120 human-labelled summaries** ([arXiv 2606.15974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15974)). **Outside that small OmniCSEval set, no paper was found with human-labelled summaries from Claude 4.x, Gemini 2.x or Llama 3.3/4.**

Two facts about English generator size and quality matter for interpretation. Pre-2023 evaluation data is almost entirely 0.1–0.6B fine-tuned encoder-decoders, while post-2023 benchmarks span roughly 3.8B–72B open models plus closed APIs. Several benchmarks keep deliberately small members, such as FaithBench's smallest-in-family picks and UniSumEval's Phi-2, Flan-T5 and BART. Newer does not mean more faithful either. In VeriGray, "Claude-3.5-Sonnet generates the fewest Unfaithful sentences (3%), surpassing the more recent GPT-5 (6%), whereas Qwen3-8B generates the most (32%)" ([arXiv 2510.21118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21118)). In TofuEval, GPT-3.5-Turbo has an 8.8% sentence-level error rate on MediaSum main topics, against 14.6–29.1% for the open models ([arXiv 2402.13249](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13249)).

## Multilingual work lags further and leans on translation

Multilingual metric papers reuse a smaller pool of old data, mainly XL-Sum with mT5 outputs, and they translate far more often than English papers do. STEMF says only that the mFACE summary "is generated by an LLM". The mT5-XXL identification comes from the mFACE paper itself. STEMF machine-translates FRANK into five languages without naming the MT system, and its results are broken down by starting model and language, **not by generator** ([arXiv 2507.20752](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20752); [ACL Anthology 2026.findings-eacl.266](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.266)). Mondshine et al. (2025), the closest Hebrew-inclusive metric meta-evaluation, use GPT-3.5-Turbo-0125 and Gemini 1.0 Pro summaries in 8 languages including Hebrew. They report correlations by language family and resource level, with no per-generator metric breakdown ([arXiv 2507.08342](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08342)).

| Dimension | English-focused | Multilingual / non-English |
|---|---|---|
| Typical metric-paper age gap | 2–4 y (up to 6 for re-adjudication of 2017–2020 data) | 1–3 y typical; **5.5–7 y** in STEMF (2026) |
| Typical benchmark-paper age gap | 0–1 y | 0–1 y (METAL, HeSum, BASSE, MSumBench, AraHalluEval, MEMERAG) |
| Freshest human-labelled summarization generators found | GPT-5, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3-8B (VeriGray); GPT-5 / Gemini 2.5 Pro in a 120-summary set (OmniCSEval) | GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini-1.5-pro (MSumBench, en/zh); Claude 3.5, GPT-4o (BASSE); GPT-4o and o3 (AraHalluEval, human annotation, no metric) |
| Frontier closed generator included | Most 2024+ benchmarks | About 10 of 26 surveyed papers; about 12 have no closed generator |
| Generator sizes | 0.1–0.6B (pre-2023), 3.8B–72B plus closed (2024+) | mT5/mBART 0.3–13B; open LLMs mostly ≤9B (Ul Islam, AraHalluEval) up to 72B |
| Translated data | Rare | Pervasive: translated test sets (STEMF/FRANK, M-RewardBench, HalluVerse-M3, RECON), translated training data (mFACT), or metrics that translate outputs to English (Multi-FActScore) |
| Metric held fixed, accuracy compared across generator eras | Yes: Tang 2023, Zhong & Litman 2025, UniSumEval | **None found** |
| Hebrew precedents | — | HeSum (GPT-4-0613, GPT-3.5, mLongT5), Mondshine (GPT-3.5-0125, Gemini 1.0 Pro), Dicta-LM 3.0 (current open models, GPT-4o judge, no human validation) |

Translation is not a neutral substitute for native data. M-RewardBench, confirmed at ACL 2025, finds that better translation quality raises reward-model scores ([ACL Anthology 2025.acl-long.3](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.3)). Thellmann et al. tie translation errors to 6–8 point accuracy drops ([arXiv 2605.24904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24904)). M-Prometheus finds that translated training data hurts ([arXiv 2504.04953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04953)). Even WMT's Hebrew data carries this caveat: "For Hebrew↔English, our references are likely to be post-edited MT" ([WMT23 General](https://aclanthology.org/2023.wmt-1.1.pdf)).

HeSum is the strongest Hebrew precedent that a metric validated on one generator class misranks another. It compares GPT-4-0613, GPT-3.5-turbo-0613 and fine-tuned mLongT5 base/large. ROUGE-1 is 17.47 for mLongT5 against 13.59 for GPT-4, while human coherence is 4.48 for GPT-4 against 3.46 for mLongT5. ROUGE correlates *negatively* with human judgments (Pearson about −0.16) ([arXiv 2406.03897](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03897)). This is a system ranking, not metric accuracy measured per generator. The 2026 multilingual summarization additions, OmniScore-Bench, BanSum and BabelJudge, do not name their annotated generators or were read only at snippet level, so none does a Tang-style analysis ([arXiv 2604.05083](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05083v1)).

## Beyond summarization, only shared tasks and leaderboards keep generators current

**Machine translation** is the positive model. The WMT23 General organizers added GPT-4 themselves ("one dominant commercial LLM (GPT4) was included via our own efforts"), and WMT23 introduced English↔Hebrew ([WMT23 General](https://aclanthology.org/2023.wmt-1.1.pdf)). The WMT23 Metrics organizers separately added NLLB-200 3.3B outputs ([WMT23 Metrics](https://aclanthology.org/2023.wmt-1.51.pdf)). WMT24 added 8 LLMs, including GPT-4, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini-1.5-Pro and Llama3-70B ([WMT24 General](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wmt-1.1.pdf)). WMT25 lists 24 organizer-collected systems, 20 of them LLMs, from AyaExpanse-8B to DeepSeek-V3 671B, plus Claude-4, Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-4.1 ([arXiv 2508.14909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.14909)). Metric papers outside the shared task still validate on last year's WMT MQM, so their gap is about 1 year. GEMBA, AutoMQM and xCOMET all use WMT22 data, and xCOMET has no GPT-4 or ChatGPT outputs even though both existed.

**Code** has no such institution. CodeBERTScore and ICE-Score validate on CoNaLa outputs (Evtikhiev's 2018–2021 systems) and code-davinci-002 HumanEval predictions ([EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.859); [arXiv 2304.14317](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.14317)). CodeJudge (EMNLP 2024) does not name its HumanEval-X/APPS generator ([arXiv 2410.02184](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02184)). Wang et al. (ISSTA 2025) use 12 open code LLMs of 34B or less, released up to Sep 2024, with GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V2.5 as judges ([arXiv 2502.06193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06193)). CodeJudgeBench (ACL 2026) deliberately generates with Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Flash/Pro, Claude-4-Sonnet/Opus and Qwen3-235B. Its stated reason is that "earlier benchmarks often rely on weaker models like GPT-4o, which produce less challenging samples" ([arXiv 2507.10535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10535)).

**Long-form factuality** is the freshest family. FActScore's human-annotated generators are InstructGPT (text-davinci-003), ChatGPT and PerplexityAI ([arXiv 2305.14251](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14251)). SAFE (NeurIPS 2024) evaluates 13 closed models up to Claude 3 Opus, but its human-agreement study reuses FActScore's 2022–23 annotations ([arXiv 2403.18802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)). FactBench regenerates "responses for existing prompts using the latest model versions" and plans annual prompt updates ([arXiv 2410.22257](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22257)). HalluLens generates test questions dynamically, with "no fixed test set" ([arXiv 2504.17550](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17550)).

**RAG and grounded QA** are 2023 snapshots: RAGAS uses ChatGPT only, ARES uses mostly mock systems, and RAGTruth uses GPT-4-0613 plus Llama-2 ([arXiv 2309.15217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15217); [arXiv 2401.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00396)). The exception is FACTS Grounding, a closed-frontier leaderboard (Gemini 2.0 Flash Exp, o1-preview, Claude 3.5) that promises to keep adding new models ([arXiv 2501.03200](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03200)).

**Judge and reward-model benchmarks** become stale through venue delay and saturation. RewardBench's AlpacaEval subsets pair outputs such as GPT-4-Turbo against Alpaca 7B, and "Some subsets are solved by small RMs, reaching 100% accuracy" ([arXiv 2403.13787](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13787v2)). RewardBench 2's pool tops out at claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022 and gpt-4o-2024-08-06, with no reasoning models as generators, and "models score about 20 points on average lower" than on v1 ([arXiv 2506.01937](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01937)).

**Dialogue and data-to-text** metrics such as UniEval, GPTScore and TIGERScore were meta-evaluated on 2015–2020 outputs, even when GPT-3 was the metric ([arXiv 2302.04166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04166)). Kasner & Dušek (ACL 2024) built fresh, contamination-free inputs but evaluated 7B open models plus GPT-3.5, with GPT-4 only as the metric ([arXiv 2401.10186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10186)).

The **additional task families** follow the same split. Instruction following is fresh: IFEval evaluates GPT-4 and PaLM 2 Small with deterministic checks ([arXiv 2311.07911](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07911)). Arena-Hard's newest generators include GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet and Llama-3.1-405B, and it argues for "continuous development of new benchmarks" ([arXiv 2406.11939](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11939)). Math step evaluation is fresh but open-only: ProcessBench uses 12 Qwen and Llama generators up to Qwen2.5 ([ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.50)). Story evaluation lags about 1 year: Chhun et al. (TACL 2024) use 2023 open models of up to 70B plus 2018–2021 HANNA systems ([arXiv 2405.13769](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13769v1)). LitBench's test texts are mostly human-written Reddit stories ([arXiv 2507.00769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00769)). Simplification ranges from LENS, built mostly on 24 past systems plus a GPT-3.5 subset ([arXiv 2212.09739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09739)), to Wu & Arase, with GPT-4, Qwen2.5-72B and Llama-3.2-3B ([arXiv 2403.04963](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04963v4)). Agent-as-a-Judge evaluates agent frameworks (MetaGPT, GPT-Pilot, OpenHands) whose backbone LLMs are not confirmed ([arXiv 2410.10934](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10934v2)).

## Degradation evidence is real, but every headline result carries a caveat

The best evidence comes from English summarization. Tang et al. (ACL 2023), verbatim: "Approximately a 10% balanced accuracy decrease on average occurs from OLD to FTSOTA" on CNN/DM. The 10% means balanced-accuracy points averaged over metrics, not a relative drop. On XSum the results are "more mixed" ([arXiv 2205.12854](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12854)). Tang et al. add that "much of the recent improvement in the factuality detection space has been on summaries from older (pre-Transformer) models". UniSumEval reports that "automated faithfulness evaluation is more challenging for summaries generated by recent LLMs compared to those generated by non-LLMs" and that NLI-based evaluators "show much lower agreement compared to G-Eval". That statistic, however, measures human corrections to LLM pre-annotation, not a standard metric's accuracy ([arXiv 2409.19898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.19898)). Ramprasad et al. (ACL 2024 main) find that detectors "struggle to detect these nuanced errors" in LLM summaries ([ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.677)). Zhong & Litman (Findings ACL 2025) is the cleanest recent fixed-evaluator comparison across eras: "advanced systems can achieve higher recall in error detection for older summaries, yet struggle with false positives and fine-grained error detection". Only the abstract-level claim is verified, and the verification notes record no URL for this paper, so locate it in the Findings of ACL 2025 proceedings before citing. The per-era numbers were not extracted.

**FaithBench needs careful citation.** Its NAACL camera-ready reports detector balanced accuracy of **48.7–62.3%**, with RAGAS using GPT-4o best at 62.31. The earlier "best 58%" is correct only for arXiv v1. The samples were *chosen* as cases "on which popular, state-of-the-art hallucination detection models, including GPT-4o-as-a-judge, disagreed" ([ACL Anthology 2025.naacl-short.38](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-short.38.pdf)), so near-chance accuracy is partly a result of how the data was selected. On the same data, FaithJudge (o3-mini-high with few-shot human examples) reaches **84% balanced accuracy** ([arXiv 2505.04847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04847)). On VeriGray, GPT-5 + RAG reaches 83.6%, while non-LLM and hidden-state detectors stay at about 47–59% ([arXiv 2510.21118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21118)). The defensible reading is that **fixed NLI-style metrics transfer poorly to modern-LLM summaries, while strong LLM judges with good prompting largely recover**.

Outside summarization the signals agree in direction but rest on weaker designs. Shelf-Life (ICLR 2026) found "no instance where judges generalize to new or stronger responses, with all FutureProof values being negative". In one example, a Mistral-24B DPO judge scores 68.56 on weak-generator responses and 65.36 on strong ones. Its tasks are **DeepScaleR math and MMLU-Pro reasoning, not summarization** ([arXiv 2509.23542](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23542)). JudgeBench's Claude-3.5-Sonnet judge falls from 64.3% on GPT-4o pairs to 44.8% on pairs it generated itself ([arXiv 2410.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12784)). In MT-Bench, GPT-4's agreement with humans rises "from 70% to nearly 100%" as the gap between the compared models widens ([arXiv 2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685)). Dorner et al. (ICLR 2025) prove that when the judge is no more accurate than the evaluated model, "no debiasing method can decrease the required amount of ground truth labels by more than half" ([arXiv 2410.13341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13341)).

In code, the mean judge accuracy in CodeJudgeBench is 62.11 on Gemini-2.5-Pro-generated pairs and 79.49 on Claude-3.7-generated pairs. **Each split, however, contains different problems**, because a pair exists only where a generator produced both correct and incorrect code. The difficulty labels are also defined by how many judges answer correctly, and the authors attribute part of the effect to "coding style or response formatting" ([arXiv 2507.10535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10535)).

MT pushes the other way. WMT24 found "no issues for neural fine-tuned metrics when evaluating LLM-based translations" ([WMT24 Metrics](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wmt-1.2.pdf)). At the same time, metrics remain "insensitive to nuanced differences" when translation quality is high ([Agrawal et al., arXiv 2405.18348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18348)), and WMT25's segment-level LLM judges were outranked by YiSi-1, chrF and BERTScore ([WMT25](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.24.pdf)). Self-preference is a separate, generator-dependent bias: +3.23% in FACTS Grounding ([arXiv 2501.03200](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03200)), and GEMBA-MQM "frequently assigns zero MQM scores to GPT-4 translations, even when humans identify errors" ([arXiv 2405.18348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18348)).

## What MorphEvalHeb should do before ARR January 2027

At ARR January 2027, MorphEvalHeb's evaluation has the following freshness. The facts about MorphEvalHeb come from the project description and the earlier synthesis, and should be checked against the paper.

| Evaluation set | Models that **generated** the evaluated outputs | Other model roles (not generators) | Age gap at 2027 | Human labels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Injected | Human/reference MAFAT summaries (2022) | LLMs inject the errors (error injectors) | ~5 y for the text; errors are synthetic, not model errors | Construction labels |
| Natural | mFACT summaries from ~2021–22-era models | GPT-4o-mini as **translator** into Hebrew | **~5–6 y**, translated | Inherited from mFACT |
| Current-model summaries | Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5.6-luna, Qwen3-32B, GLM-4.7-flash, Ministral-3-14B, DictaLM 3 | — | ~0 y | **None yet** |

**The reviewer risk is concrete.** As they stand, the headline numbers match the SummaC → FineSurE → STEMF pattern: a new metric validated on old, synthetic or translated outputs. Tang 2023, UniSumEval, Ramprasad 2024 and Zhong & Litman 2025 give a reviewer verified grounds to ask whether accuracy holds on current LLM Hebrew summaries. The Natural set has two staleness problems at once. Its generators are old, and its outputs are machine-translated, which multilingual work shows biases evaluation (M-RewardBench, Thellmann, M-Prometheus). The field norm does partly protect the paper. STEMF was accepted at EACL Findings 2026 with an older test set, so old data alone is not disqualifying, but it is a recognized weakness that reviewers can raise.

**First recommendation: human-label a slice of the current-model summaries and report every metric per generator.** On the searches done, no multilingual paper holds one metric fixed across older and newer generators. A Hebrew comparison of the Natural (about 2021–22) outputs against 2025–26 outputs would be a contribution, not just a defence. State this as "to our knowledge", because the absence rests on bounded searches. Precedents support a small set. OmniCSEval validated its judge on 120 human-labelled summaries. FaithBench used 660 summaries from 10 LLMs. Keep at least one small or weak member alongside the frontier ones, as FaithBench and UniSumEval did, so that generator strength varies within the set. Report hallucination rate per generator. VeriGray found GPT-5 less faithful than Claude-3.5-Sonnet, so do not assume that newer means fewer errors.

**Second: publish a freshness table that keeps roles separate.** For every set, list generator, release date, size, open or closed, and native or translated, and list injectors, translators and judges in separate columns. This costs little, pre-empts the critique, and follows the generator-versus-judge distinction that the surveyed literature often blurs.

**Third: avoid same-family loops.** Generator, injector, translator and judge from one family can inflate scores. The evidence: FACTS Grounding's +3.23% self-preference; GEMBA-MQM's zero-error scores on GPT-4 translations; K-HALU and HalluVerse-M3, where the model that built the data was the best detector; and WMT25, where judges are the same models as translators. If GPT-family models judge the GPT-5.6-luna summaries, or translated the Natural set, report the per-family results.

**Fourth: include a strong LLM-judge baseline on the current slice, and cite carefully.** On modern-LLM summaries, NLI-style detectors sit near 47–59% balanced accuracy, while well-prompted frontier judges reach about 84% (FaithJudge, VeriGray). MorphEvalHeb's morphology-specific value should therefore be shown against an LLM judge on current outputs, not only against NLI baselines on old ones.

When citing the evidence:
- Give FaithBench as 48.7–62.3%, with its adversarial-selection caveat.
- Describe Shelf-Life as reasoning-task evidence extrapolated to summarization.
- Describe CodeJudgeBench's split gap as confounded by problem mix.
- For a Hebrew MT precedent, use the WMT23 Metrics MQM he→en table (GPT-4 1.33, behind the human reference at 1.17, narrowly ahead of ONLINE-A at 1.38), noting that the Hebrew references are likely post-edited MT.
- Use HeSum as the Hebrew evidence that a metric can misrank generator classes.

## Conclusion

The verification pass changes how the evidence should be used more than what it concludes. The direction holds: in summarization, metrics validated on older or weaker generators look better than they are on current ones. But the most quotable results are narrower than the first pass suggested. FaithBench's near-chance scores were partly produced by how its samples were selected, Shelf-Life measures reasoning, CodeJudgeBench's splits mix generator with problem, and WMT24 found MT metrics robust. The cleanest summarization-specific support remains Tang 2023, UniSumEval, Ramprasad 2024 and Zhong & Litman 2025, and all of it is English. The failure the evidence actually supports is that *fixed, non-LLM* faithfulness metrics degrade on modern outputs while strong LLM judges largely do not. A Hebrew metric that claims morphological sensitivity therefore has to beat a strong LLM judge on current-model Hebrew outputs, not only NLI baselines on 2021–22 outputs.

Generator freshness is an institutional property, not an individual virtue. It persists only where someone refreshes the outputs every year, as WMT does. Summarization faithfulness, and multilingual summarization in particular, has no such mechanism. MorphEvalHeb already has current-model Hebrew summaries in hand. A modest human-labelled slice of them, analysed per generator next to the older Natural set, would turn the paper's largest reviewer liability into the first multilingual test of whether a factual-consistency metric holds across generator eras, a question no multilingual paper has yet answered.
