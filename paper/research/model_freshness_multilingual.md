# Model freshness in multilingual / non-English evaluation papers

Produced 2026-11-18 by a research subagent. Scope: multilingual and non-English metrics and benchmarks. Summarization
comes first, then hallucination evaluation and LLM-judge or reward-model meta-evaluation. Companion files:
`model_freshness_en_summarization.md`, `model_freshness_qa_rag_judges.md`, `model_freshness_mt_code.md`.

**How facts were checked.**
- (v) = verified from the paper's full text (PDF downloaded from arXiv, ACL Anthology or ICLR proceedings and searched
  this session).
- (mem) = from memory, not re-checked here.
- (unverified) = could not be confirmed.
- Model release dates are first public release (mem). These are standard dates, but check any date you quote.

**Definitions.**
- **Gen** = systems whose outputs make up the evaluation data, i.e. the outputs that are labelled or scored.
- **Judge** = LLMs or models used *as* the metric, evaluator, teacher or data synthesiser.
- **Gap** = paper date (first arXiv version, or venue if there was no preprint) minus the release date of the newest
  Gen, in years.
- **Frontier?** = whether the strongest closed model available at the paper's date is among the Gen.

## Patterns

**1. Typical age gap: bimodal.**
- **Multilingual summarisation-faithfulness *metric* papers lag about 1–5 years, median ~2.2.** Examples: mFACE ~2.2,
  mFACT validation ~2.8, SEAHORSE ~1.1, Gao 2023 ~3, Kang 2024 ~1.3, Mondshine ~1.5, STEMF ~4.8.
  - The cause is reuse. XL-Sum and mT5 outputs from 2020–22 are the de facto multilingual test bed.
  - **STEMF (EACL 2026) still validates on mFACE's mT5-XXL summaries and on translated FRANK (2019–20 systems).** It
    trains 2024 Qwen2.5 and Gemma-2 judges, so the judges are about 4–5 years newer than the summaries they judge.
- **Benchmark and meta-evaluation papers from 2024 on are fresh (gap 0–1).** METAL, HeSum, BASSE, MSumBench,
  AraHalluEval, MEMERAG, MM-Eval, MultiHal, HalluQA and Multi-FActScore all generate new outputs for the paper.
- Median across all 26 rows with real generator outputs: about 0.6 years. Excluding new-benchmark papers it is
  about 1.5–2 years.

**2. Frontier and closed models.**
- About 10 of 26 include the closed frontier model of their time as a generator: Multi-FAct, METAL, Hada'24, HeSum,
  HalluQA, MM-Eval, BASSE, MSumBench, AraHalluEval, and RECON (synthetic).
- About 4 include only mid-tier closed models: SEAHORSE (PaLM), MEMERAG and MultiHal (mini / Flash), and Dicta-LM 3
  (as the reference).
- About 12 use no closed generator at all: mFACE, mFACT, Gao, Kang, Ul Islam, Chataigner, Mu-SHROOM,
  MultiWikiQHalluA, STEMF, Mondshine, and Thellmann's downstream models.
- Hallucination-rate studies tend to use open models only, sometimes deliberately (Mu-SHROOM needs logits).
- **Judges are almost always closed and newest.** Doğruöz 2026 finds GPT-family judges in 79% of 33 multilingual
  judge papers.

**3. Generator sizes.**
- Pre-2023: mT5 or mBART encoder-decoders (0.3–13B). PaLM-540B and BLOOMZ-176B are the large exceptions.
- 2024–26 open-model studies: mostly ≤9B (Ul Islam caps at 9B; AraHalluEval's Arabic models are 6.7–9B) up to
  70B. Rarely above 72B except via closed APIs.

**4. Translated vs native outputs. Translation is pervasive, but at different points in the pipeline.**
- **Native generated outputs:** mFACE, SEAHORSE, METAL, Hada'24, HeSum, BASSE, Mondshine, AraHalluEval, MEMERAG,
  Mu-SHROOM, HalluQA, K-HALU.
- **Translated evaluation data:** M-RewardBench, RECON, HalluVerse-M3, Poly-FEVER, MultiHal (questions),
  LCHAIM, translated FRANK in STEMF, and EU20 in Thellmann.
- **Translated training data:** mFACT, Ul Islam's detectors.
- **Translated sources only:** MSumBench (summaries still native).
- **The metric itself translates outputs to English:** Multi-FActScore and Chataigner (FActScore-style), Gao 2023.
- Several papers show that translation biases results:
  - M-RewardBench: translation quality changes RM scores.
  - MM-Eval: translated benchmarks fail to align with human-curated ones.
  - Thellmann: 6–8 pp accuracy drops from translation errors.
  - M-Prometheus: translated training data hurts.

**5. Breakdowns of metric quality by generator, model strength or resource level.**
- **By generator:**
  - HeSum (Hebrew): ROUGE prefers fine-tuned mLongT5 while humans prefer GPT-4, with negative ROUGE–human
    correlation. This shows directly that a metric validated on one generator class misranks another.
  - MSumBench: self-preference varies by judge/generator pair.
  - K-HALU and HalluVerse-M3: the model that built the data is the best detector.
- **By resource level (very common):**
  - mFACE: faithfulness worst for Hausa and Yoruba.
  - Hada'24: score inflation in low-resource and non-Latin-script languages.
  - MM-Eval: −12.8 and −18.4 pp in low-resource languages; good responses scored lower.
  - Fu & Liu: κ lowest for low-resource languages; scale does not help.
  - Kang: NLI fails for lower-resource languages.
  - Multi-FAct: the GPT-4 vs GPT-3.5 gap is largest in low-resource languages.
- **By judge strength:** BASSE finds closed judges ≫ open judges, with no gain from larger open judges.
- **No multilingual paper found tests one fixed metric on old vs new generators.** The closest is SEAHORSE's
  per-system rating table, which reports human ratings and not metric accuracy. The English AggreFact finding
  (Tang et al. 2023) has no multilingual analogue.

**6. Do multilingual papers lag English ones?**
- For *metric* papers, yes, slightly or comparably.
  - The English companion report finds a typical 2–4-year gap, driven by reusing FRANK, SummaC and AggreFact.
  - Multilingual metric papers show the same mechanism with a smaller pool of reusable data (mFACE/XL-Sum, SEAHORSE).
  - A 2026 multilingual metric is still validated on 2020 mT5 summaries (STEMF).
- For *new benchmarks*, multilingual work is as fresh as English (gap 0–1). MSumBench appears in both lists.
- Multilingual benchmarks rely much more on translated data. That is a confound English benchmarks do not have.

**7. Implications for MorphEvalHeb.**
- Hebrew precedent:
  - HeSum: GPT-4-0613 and GPT-3.5, gap ~1.
  - Mondshine: GPT-3.5-0125 and Gemini 1.0 Pro, gap ~1.5.
  - Dicta-LM 3: current open models, but a 2024 GPT-4o judge.
- Testing on outputs from current (2025–26) models would put MorphEvalHeb ahead of every multilingual metric paper in
  this table (none has a gap under 1 year). Testing on older outputs would be in line with field practice, especially
  if they are native-Hebrew generated rather than translated.
- Useful citations for a "freshness" footnote:
  - STEMF (mT5 2020 test data in 2026).
  - HeSum (metric misranking across generator classes).
  - Doğruöz 2026 (judge concentration).
  - MM-Eval and Hada'24 (low-resource miscalibration).
