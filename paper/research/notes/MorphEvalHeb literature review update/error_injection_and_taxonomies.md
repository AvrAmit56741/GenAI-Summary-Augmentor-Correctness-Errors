# Synthetic error injection, factual-error taxonomies, morphology-aware perturbation, and claim decomposition (lit-review update for MorphEvalHeb, as of 2026-11-12)

Verification note: every entry below was checked against its ACL Anthology page/bibtex (author lists pulled from the Anthology .bib) or the arXiv API record (authors, first-submission date, comments field). "PR" = peer-reviewed (Anthology / journal / conference listing confirmed); "PR (arXiv comment)" = venue stated only in the arXiv comments field, no proceedings page opened; "arXiv-only" = no venue found. Excluded as already cited: FactCC, FactKB, Goyal & Durrett 2020 (DAE), Cao et al. 2022, Maynez et al. 2020, MiniCheck, AlignScore.

Recommendation codes: (i) = cite in related work; (ii) = add as a baseline/comparison.

## Q1. What are the closest prior works to the paper's error-injection pipeline, and how do they differ?

### Takeaway
The closest prior works are the rule-based perturbation family (CLIFF, FactPEGASUS, FactEdit, AMRFact; FactCC/DAE already cited) and LLM-based "decompose → falsify an atomic claim → recompose" pipelines (LLM-Oasis 2026, FAVA, HAD, FRED, FactCG). None of them injects errors that are morphology-specific (binyan, construct state, gender agreement), and none targets Hebrew. TrueTeacher is the main counter-paradigm: it labels real model summaries with an LLM instead of perturbing gold summaries, and it explicitly argues that perturbed data fails to match real errors. The paper should cite it and address that argument.

### Cited Findings
**Rule/structure-based negative generation (pre-LLM lineage)**
- **CLIFF**: Shuyang Cao, Lu Wang. "CLIFF: Contrastive Learning for Improving Faithfulness and Factuality in Abstractive Summarization." EMNLP 2021, pp. 6633–6649. PR. Builds negative summaries by entity swap, mask-and-fill, source-conditioned regeneration, and low-confidence model generations, then uses them for contrastive training of the summarizer. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.532/). Relevance: same family of negative-construction strategies as entity swap and few-shot generation, but it trains a generator, not a detector. **(i)**
- **FactPEGASUS**: David Wan, Mohit Bansal. "FactPEGASUS: Factuality-Aware Pre-training and Fine-tuning for Abstractive Summarization." NAACL 2022, pp. 1010–1028. PR. Uses contrastive learning with perturbed negatives (entity-based corruption). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.74/). **(i)**
- **FactEdit**: Vidhisha Balachandran, Hannaneh Hajishirzi, William Cohen, Yulia Tsvetkov. "Correcting Diverse Factual Errors in Abstractive Summarization via Post-Editing and Language Model Infilling." EMNLP 2022, pp. 9818–9830. PR. Generates hard, diverse factual errors (entity, predicate, circumstance types modelled on FRANK) by LM infilling to train a corrector. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.667/). Relevance: it ties synthetic error generation to the FRANK error types, as MorphEvalHeb ties injection operators to taxonomy families. **(i)**
- **AMRFact**: Haoyi Qiu, Kung-Hsiang Huang, Jingnong Qu, Nanyun Peng. "AMRFact: Enhancing Summarization Factuality Evaluation with AMR-Driven Negative Samples Generation." NAACL 2024 per the arXiv comment (arXiv:2311.09521). PR (arXiv comment); I did not resolve the Anthology ID. The abstract says earlier perturbed summaries "are either of low coherence or lack error-type coverage". AMRFact perturbs AMR graphs per FRANK-style error type, generates text from them, and filters invalid negatives with an NLI + BARTScore module. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09521). Relevance: the **closest structural analogue**, because it has typed controlled perturbations, regeneration, and a validity filter, matching MorphEvalHeb's decompose/inject/recompose/filter. It is English-only and has no AMR parser for Hebrew. **(ii)** if an English-side comparison is feasible, otherwise **(i)**.
- **DAE fine-grained**: Tanya Goyal, Greg Durrett. "Annotating and Modeling Fine-grained Factuality in Summarization." NAACL 2021, pp. 1449–1462. PR. Shows that synthetic (entity/generation-based) training data transfers poorly to real errors on XSum and proposes arc-level annotations. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.114/). This is a different paper from the already-cited Goyal & Durrett 2020. **(i)**

**LLM-labelled / LLM-generated synthetic training data (2023–2026)**
- **TrueTeacher**: Zorik Gekhman, Jonathan Herzig, Roee Aharoni, Chen Elkind, Idan Szpektor. "TrueTeacher: Learning Factual Consistency Evaluation with Large Language Models." EMNLP 2023, pp. 2053–2070. PR. Argues that synthetic data from perturbing human-written summaries "fails to match real model outputs and misses many error types". Instead, an LLM labels diverse model-generated summaries. Releases 1.4M examples, notes the method is multilingual, and its student beats the LLM teacher on TRUE. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.127/). Relevance: the main methodological alternative and the strongest critique of perturbation data. **(i) (essential)**. Also **(ii)** as a data-source baseline: train NeoDictaBERT on TrueTeacher-style LLM-labelled Hebrew summaries instead of injected errors.
- **LLM-Oasis**: Alessandro Scirè, Andrei Stefan Bejgu, Simone Tedeschi, Karim Ghonim, Federico Martelli, Roberto Navigli. "Truth or Mirage? Towards End-To-End Factuality Evaluation with LLM-Oasis." *Computational Linguistics* 52(1), 2026. PR (journal; arXiv:2411.19655, Nov 2024). An LLM extracts atomic claims from Wikipedia passages, falsifies **one** claim, and generates a factual paraphrase plus an unfactual text, giving about 81k pairs. Small models trained on it are competitive; GPT-4o reaches only about 60% on end-to-end evaluation. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.cl-1.1/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19655), [search summary of method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19655v3). Relevance: the **closest pipeline match** (atomic claims → single controlled falsification → recomposition, plus paraphrase positives). It differs in being English, LLM-only falsification with no linguistic operators, and binary labels only. **(i) (essential)**
- **FAVA**: Abhika Mishra, Akari Asai, Vidhisha Balachandran, Yizhong Wang, Graham Neubig, Yulia Tsvetkov, Hannaneh Hajishirzi. "Fine-grained Hallucination Detection and Editing for Language Models." COLM 2024 per the arXiv comment (arXiv:2401.06855). PR (arXiv comment). Prompts ChatGPT/GPT-4 with few-shot examples to **insert errors of specified types** with tagged spans, then trains a detector/editor (Llama2-7B). Uses a 6-type hierarchical taxonomy (entity, relation, contradictory, invented, subjective, unverifiable). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06855), [project page](https://fine-grained-hallucination.github.io). Relevance: typed LLM error insertion that yields type labels for free, which is the same idea as MorphEvalHeb's few-shot LLM injection. **(i)**
- **HAD**: Fan Xu, Xinyu Hu, Zhenghan Yu, Li Lin, Xu Zhang, Yang Zhang, Wei Zhou, Jinjie Gu, Xiaojun Wan. "HAD: HAllucination Detection Language Models Based on a Comprehensive Hallucination Taxonomy." arXiv:2510.19318, 22 Oct 2025. arXiv-only (no venue listed). Proposes an 11-category taxonomy across NLG tasks. Models trained on about 90K synthetic samples perform detection, span identification, and correction together; HADTest has 2,248 annotated samples. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19318). **(i)**; possibly **(ii)** (the released models are at github.com/pku0xff/HAD, but they are English/Chinese).
- **FRED**: Likun Tan, Kuan-Wei Huang, Kevin Wu. "FRED: Financial Retrieval-Enhanced Detection and Editing of Hallucinations in Language Models." arXiv:2507.20930, Jul 2025. arXiv-only. Builds a synthetic dataset by inserting **tagged errors from a user-defined domain taxonomy** into financial QA, then fine-tunes Phi-4/Qwen3 detectors (+8% binary F1 for Phi-4). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20930). Relevance: a taxonomy-driven insertion recipe. **(i)**
- **FactCG**: Deren Lei, Yaxi Li, Siyao Li, Mengya Hu, Rui Xu, Ken Archer, Mingyu Wang, Emily Ching, Alex Deng. "FactCG: Enhancing Fact Checkers with Graph-Based Multi-Hop Data." NAACL 2025 (Long), pp. 5002–5020. PR. The CG2C method extracts a context graph, samples multi-hop subgraphs, and generates claims; negatives come from graph edits. Reported to outperform GPT-4o on LLM-AggreFact with a much smaller model. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.258/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17144); the negative-construction detail comes from a [secondary review](https://liner.com/review/factcg-enhancing-fact-checkers-with-graphbased-multihop-data) and should be checked in the paper. Relevance: the "MiniCheck-successor" synthetic-data verifier. **(i)**, and **(ii)** if a multilingual checkpoint can be applied (zero-shot cross-lingual baseline).
- **HaluEval**: Junyi Li, Xiaoxue Cheng, Wayne Xin Zhao, Jian-Yun Nie, Ji-Rong Wen. "HaluEval: A Large-Scale Hallucination Evaluation Benchmark for Large Language Models." EMNLP 2023, pp. 6449–6464. PR. Uses a "sample-then-filter" LLM generation of hallucinated QA, dialogue, and summarization outputs. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.397/). Relevance: LLM-generated negatives with a filtering step, analogous to MorphEvalHeb's similarity filter. **(i)**
- **HalluDial**: Wen Luo, Tianshu Shen, Wei Li, Guangyue Peng, Richeng Xuan, Houfeng Wang, Xi Yang. "HalluDial: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Automatic Dialogue-Level Hallucination Evaluation." arXiv:2406.07070, Jun 2024. arXiv-only as far as I verified. Covers dialogue-level hallucination with localization and rationales, and both factuality and faithfulness hallucinations. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07070). **(i)** (low priority)

**Perturbation as stress-test (not training), 2026**
- **Stress testing factuality metrics**: Zain Muhammad Mujahid, Dustin Wright, Isabelle Augenstein. "Stress Testing Factual Consistency Metrics for Long-Document Summarization." ACL 2026 (Long). PR. Applies seven **meaning-preserving** perturbations (paraphrase, simplification, synonym replacement, logically equivalent negation, vocabulary reduction, compression, source insertion) to six reference-free metrics. Per the search summary, AlignScore and SummaC-ZS are especially unreliable. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1472/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07689). Relevance: directly supports MorphEvalHeb's back-translation/paraphrase **positives**, since metrics wrongly penalise meaning-preserving rewrites. **(i)**
- **Preference learning from multiple metrics**: Yuxuan Ye, Raul Santos-Rodriguez, Edwin Simpson. "Optimising Factual Consistency in Summarisation via Preference Learning from Multiple Imperfect Metrics." arXiv:2605.26840 (v1 dated 2026-05-26). The arXiv comment says "EMNLP 2025 Findings", which is odd against a 2026 arXiv date; I did not confirm it on the Anthology. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26840). Peripheral (generation side). Optional **(i)**.

### Inferences
- Positioning sentence: MorphEvalHeb = LLM-Oasis/FAVA-style claim-level controlled falsification + FactCC/CLIFF/AMRFact-style deterministic operators + **a morphological operator family that no prior injection pipeline has**. Prior operators work on entities, predicates, numbers, negation, or AMR/graph structure. None manipulates inflectional or derivational morphology (binyan, construct state, gender/number agreement), and these are the error sources specific to morphologically rich languages.
- The paper should pre-empt the TrueTeacher and Goyal & Durrett 2021 critique (perturbed negatives misrepresent real errors) by evaluating on **naturally occurring** Hebrew summary errors, not only held-out synthetic ones.
- Typed injection (FAVA, FRED, HAD, AMRFact, MorphEvalHeb) gives multi-label supervision for free, which is the link between Q1 and Q3.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 synthetic-negative factuality work targeting Hebrew, or any morphologically rich language, with morphology-aware operators. That is a gap in the literature as far as this search could tell, not proof it does not exist.
- I did not open the full FactCG and AMRFact PDFs, so the operator-level details (edge removal; AMR perturbation types) rest on abstracts and a secondary review.
- I did not find a separately published "MiniCheck successor" beyond FactCG, nor a peer-reviewed venue for HAD, FRED, or HalluDial.

## Q2. Which fine-grained taxonomies should the 24-family taxonomy be positioned against?

### Takeaway
The canonical summarization taxonomies are FRANK (semantic-frame, discourse, and content-verifiability errors), Goyal & Durrett 2021 (arc-level), AggreFact/Tang et al. 2023 (intrinsic/extrinsic × noun-phrase/predicate), DiaSumFact (6 classes for dialogue), FineSurE (LLM-based fact-checking error categories), and TofuEval (dialogue, with explanations). LLM-era taxonomies to add are FAVA (6 types), HAD (11 types), RAGTruth (span-level, evident vs. subtle conflict and baseless info), and LongNovel 2026 (8 summarization types). None of them has morphosyntactic categories, so MorphEvalHeb's 24 families are much finer and add a morphology axis.

### Cited Findings
- **FRANK**: Artidoro Pagnoni, Vidhisha Balachandran, Yulia Tsvetkov. "Understanding Factuality in Abstractive Summarization with FRANK: A Benchmark for Factuality Metrics." NAACL 2021, pp. 4812–4829. PR. A linguistically grounded typology covering semantic-frame errors (predicate, entity, circumstance), discourse errors (coreference, discourse link), and content-verifiability errors (out-of-article, grammatical). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.383/). The category list comes from my knowledge of the paper; the Anthology page confirmed title, authors, and venue. **(i) (essential: the primary anchor)**. FRANK's "grammatical error" and "discourse link" categories are the nearest neighbours of MorphEvalHeb's morphology and connective families.
- **AggreFact / Tang et al.**: Liyan Tang, Tanya Goyal, Alex Fabbri, Philippe Laban, Jiacheng Xu, Semih Yavuz, Wojciech Kryściński, Justin Rousseau, Greg Durrett. "Understanding Factual Errors in Summarization: Errors, Summarizers, Datasets, Error Detectors." ACL 2023, pp. 11626–11644. PR. Unifies nine datasets into AggreFact and maps their error labels to a shared intrinsic/extrinsic × noun-phrase/predicate typology. FineGrainFact uses the same four-type scheme ([arXiv 2305.14548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14548)). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.650/). **(i) (essential)**
- **DiaSumFact**: Rongxin Zhu, Jianzhong Qi, Jey Han Lau. "Annotating and Detecting Fine-grained Factual Errors for Dialogue Summarization." ACL 2023, pp. 6825–6845. PR. Sentence-level multi-label annotation over **six** error classes; per the search summary, two SOTA models reach about 0.25 macro-F1. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.377/). **(i)**, plus **(ii)**-style reference point for multi-label difficulty.
- **FineSurE**: Hwanjun Song, Hang Su, Igor Shalyminov, Jason Cai, Saab Mansour. "FineSurE: Fine-grained Summarization Evaluation using LLMs." ACL 2024, pp. 906–922. PR. LLM-based fact checking that assigns each summary sentence an error category, alongside completeness and conciseness. I did not confirm the exact category list on the page. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.51/). **(i)**, and **(ii)** as a prompted-LLM type-level baseline (see Q3).
- **TofuEval**: Liyan Tang, Igor Shalyminov, Amy Wing-mei Wong, Jon Burnsky, Jake W. Vincent, Yu'an Yang, Siffi Singh, Song Feng, Hwanjun Song, Hang Su, Lijia Sun, Yi Zhang, Saab Mansour, Kathleen McKeown. "TofuEval: Evaluating Hallucinations of LLMs on Topic-Focused Dialogue Summarization." NAACL 2024 per the arXiv comment (arXiv:2402.13249). PR (arXiv comment). Binary sentence-level labels with explanations of the errors, plus an error-type analysis. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13249). **(i)**
- **RAGTruth**: Cheng Niu, Yuanhao Wu, Juno Zhu, Siliang Xu, KaShun Shum, Randy Zhong, Juntong Song, Tong Zhang. "RAGTruth: A Hallucination Corpus for Developing Trustworthy Retrieval-Augmented Language Models." ACL 2024, pp. 10862–10878. PR. Word/span-level annotation over summarization, QA, and data-to-text. Its types (evident/subtle conflict, evident/subtle baseless information) come from my knowledge of the paper, not from the verified page; a secondary GitHub list describes them differently, so check before citing the exact names. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.585/). **(i)**
- **FAVA taxonomy** (6 hierarchical types: entity, relation, contradictory, invented, subjective, unverifiable). See Q1. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06855). **(i)**
- **HAD taxonomy** (11 categories across NLG tasks). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19318). **(i)**
- **LongNovel**: Ruizhi Zhang, Jinwei Chen, Xiangju Lu, He Yan, Mo Yu, Junmin Zhu, Wei Zhang. "LongNovel: A Multi-Scale Benchmark for Hallucination Detection in Long-Context Novel Summarization." arXiv:2608.18082. The arXiv v2 comment says "Accepted at EMNLP 2026". The API lists the v1 date as 2026-06-04, which does not fit an 08-series ID, so treat the date with care. Chinese/English novel summarization. Per the search snippet (not confirmed in the paper), its 8 types are entity, numerical, relation, logical inversion, event, temporal, causal, and event fabrication. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18082). Relevance: the most recent (2026) summarization-specific hallucination taxonomy. **(i)**
- **Prompted multi-label typing**: Aniket Deroy, Subhankar Maity, Saptarshi Ghosh. "Prompted Zero-Shot Multi-label Classification of Factual Incorrectness in Machine-Generated Summaries." arXiv:2312.01087 (FIRE 2023 per a ResearchGate listing; not verified on a proceedings page). Four types: misrepresentation, inaccurate quantities/measurements, false attribution, fabrication. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01087). **(i)** (minor)
- **HeSum**: Tzuf Paz-Argaman, Itai Mondshine, Asaf Achi Mordechai, Reut Tsarfaty. "HeSum: a Novel Dataset for Abstractive Text Summarization in Hebrew." Findings of ACL 2024, pp. 6378–6388. PR. 10k Hebrew news article–summary pairs. A secondary review reports a qualitative error analysis including hallucinations and **morphological errors**; I did not confirm this in the paper. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.381/), [secondary review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/hesum-a-novel-dataset-for-abstractive-text-summarization-in-hebrew). Relevance: the most direct Hebrew prior, and a likely source of real (non-synthetic) Hebrew error examples for evaluating the detector. **(i) (essential if not already cited)**

### Inferences
- Suggested positioning table columns: taxonomy | #types | granularity (summary/sentence/span) | language | morphology-aware? Every prior row has "no" in the morphology column. A mapping from the 24 families to FRANK's categories and to Tang et al.'s intrinsic/extrinsic × NP/predicate types would make the taxonomy comparable and would let MorphEvalHeb types be collapsed onto AggreFact for cross-benchmark evaluation.
- The prior taxonomies have 4–11 types. 24 families is unusually fine, so the paper should report inter-annotator agreement and per-family support. DiaSumFact's ~0.25 macro-F1 over 6 classes shows how hard fine-grained typing is.

### Gaps
- I did not open the full FineSurE, RAGTruth, or LongNovel papers to confirm exact category names. Treat those category lists as "to verify" before quoting them.
- I found no Hebrew (or other Semitic-language) factual-error taxonomy for summarization.

## Q3. Is there 2025–2026 work on multi-label/type-level factual error detection that could be a baseline?

### Takeaway
Few trained, released type-level detectors exist. The usable ones are FineGrainFact (2023, multi-label over 4 types, code released), HAD (2025, 11-type detection + span + correction, code released), FAVA (2024, 6-type tagger), and prompted-LLM typing (FineSurE-style prompts, Deroy et al.). The 2025–2026 span-level work (PsiloQA multilingual, SHAM-Bench, LongNovel) mostly marks spans without types, or types them only for evaluation. The most practical baselines for a Hebrew multi-label detector are therefore: (a) a prompted LLM given the 24-family definitions (FineSurE-style), (b) FineGrainFact-style multi-label head reimplemented on NeoDictaBERT, and (c) zero-shot binary factuality models such as FactCG or TrueTeacher-style multilingual checkpoints for the binary task.

### Cited Findings
- **FineGrainFact**: Hou Pong Chan, Qi Zeng, Heng Ji. "Interpretable Automatic Fine-grained Inconsistency Detection in Text Summarization." Findings of ACL 2023 per the arXiv comment (arXiv:2305.14548). PR (arXiv comment). Defines the **fine-grained inconsistency detection** task (predict which error types are present). Its model uses SRL semantic frames and the 4-type typology of Tang et al. Code: github.com/kenchan0226/fineGrainedFact. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14548). **(ii) (strongest direct baseline/task-formulation precedent)**
- **HAD** (2025): integrated hallucination detection, span identification, and correction over 11 categories, trained on ~90K synthetic samples. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19318). **(ii)** as an LLM-based type-level comparison, run as-is or via translation.
- **FAVA** (2024): a fine-grained 6-type tagger trained on LLM-inserted typed errors. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06855). **(ii)** (optional)
- **DiaSumFact** (2023): reports about 0.25 macro-F1 for 6-class multi-label error detection (per the search summary). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.377/). Use as a difficulty reference point.
- **PsiloQA**: Elisei Rykov, Kseniia Petrushina, Maksim Savkin, Valerii Olisov, Artem Vazhentsev, Kseniia Titova, Alexander Panchenko, Vasily Konovalov, Julia Belikova. "When Models Lie, We Learn: Multilingual Span-Level Hallucination Detection with PsiloQA." arXiv:2510.04849, Oct 2025. arXiv-only as far as verified. Automatically built span-level hallucination data in **14 languages** (GPT-4o QA generation, then eliciting hallucinations from diverse LLMs, then automatic span annotation). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04849). Relevance: a recent multilingual synthetic-data precedent; spans are untyped. I did not check whether Hebrew is among the 14 languages. **(i)**
- **SHAM-Bench**: Sicong Huang, Ian Lane. "SHAM-Bench: A Span-level Hallucination Benchmark Across Multiple Tasks." arXiv:2610.27370, 19 Oct 2026. arXiv-only. Unifies 6,461 human-annotated examples across six NLG tasks; summarization is about 41% per the search summary. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27370). **(i)** (very recent; context for span-level evaluation)
- **FactSelfCheck**: Albert Sawczyn, Jakub Binkowski, Denis Janiak, Bogdan Gabrys, Tomasz Kajdanowicz. "FactSelfCheck: Fact-Level Black-Box Hallucination Detection for LLMs." Findings of EACL 2026 per the arXiv comment (arXiv:2503.17229). Represents text as knowledge-graph triples and scores fact-level hallucinations with no training data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17229). **(i)** (fact-level, not type-level)
- **mFACE**: Roee Aharoni, Shashi Narayan, Joshua Maynez, Jonathan Herzig, Elizabeth Clark, Mirella Lapata. "Multilingual Summarization with Factual Consistency Evaluation." Findings of ACL 2023, pp. 3562–3591. PR. Multilingual (45 languages, XL-Sum) factual-consistency evaluation; whether Hebrew is included was not verified. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.220/). **(i)**; **(ii)** if its multilingual NLI-based scorer can be run on Hebrew.

### Inferences
- I found no 2025–2026 *summarization-specific*, *multi-label*, *trained, released* type detector for any non-English language. MorphEvalHeb's multi-label NeoDictaBERT detector would be novel on that axis.
- For a fair comparison, MorphEvalHeb predictions can be collapsed onto the 4 Tang et al. types to compare with FineGrainFact, and onto FAVA's or HAD's types, which sidesteps the mismatch between 24 families and 4–11 types.

### Gaps
- No 2026 multi-label typed detector trained on summarization data turned up. The search for "multi-label factual error type classification summarization 2026" returned only 2023 work.
- I did not verify whether HAD or FAVA checkpoints handle Hebrew input.

## Q4 (scope item 3). Morphology-aware / linguistically grounded perturbation and minimal-pair resources for non-English languages

### Takeaway
Morphology-aware minimal pairs exist for **grammaticality** probing: MultiBLiMP (101 languages, including Hebrew subject–verb agreement), TurBLiMP (Turkish morphology), RuBLiMP (Russian), and HeCLiMP (Hebrew determiner–noun gender/number agreement). Arabic agreement-violating attacks exist for robustness testing. **None** uses morphology to create *factual-consistency* negatives, and that is MorphEvalHeb's niche. MorphEvalHeb should explicitly separate meaning-changing morphological edits (e.g., a binyan swap that changes the agent or voice) from grammaticality-only edits (agreement breaks), because the latter test fluency, not factuality.

### Cited Findings
- **MultiBLiMP 1.0**: Jaap Jumelet, Leonie Weissweiler, Joakim Nivre, Arianna Bisazza. "MultiBLiMP 1.0: A Massively Multilingual Benchmark of Linguistic Minimal Pairs." TACL (MIT Press), 2025. PR. More than 128,000 subject–verb agreement minimal pairs (number/person/gender) over 101 languages, generated automatically from Universal Dependencies + UniMorph; Hebrew is included according to the search summary. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02768), [TACL page](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/TACL.a.600/134999/MultiBLiMP-1-0-A-Massively-Multilingual-Benchmark). Relevance: an automated UD/UniMorph-driven morphological perturbation pipeline, a methodological sibling of the rule-based Hebrew operators. **(i) (essential)**
- **TurBLiMP**: Ezgi Başar, Francesca Padovani, Jaap Jumelet, Arianna Bisazza. "TurBLiMP: A Turkish Benchmark of Linguistic Minimal Pairs." EMNLP 2025 (arXiv journal_ref). PR. 16 phenomena × 1000 pairs, focused on word-order flexibility and morphological subordination; LMs' sensitivity to morphological complexity diverges from humans'. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13487). **(i)**
- **RuBLiMP**: Ekaterina Taktasheva, Maxim Bazhukov, Kirill Koncha, Alena Fenogenova, Ekaterina Artemova, Vladislav Mikhailov. "RuBLiMP: Russian Benchmark of Linguistic Minimal Pairs." EMNLP 2024, pp. 9268–9299. PR. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.522/). **(i)**
- **TafBERTa / HeCLiMP**: Anita Gelboim, Elior Sulem. "TafBERTa: Learning Grammatical Rules from Small-Scale Language Acquisition Data in Hebrew." Proceedings of the First BabyLM Workshop, 2025, pp. 76–90. PR (workshop). Introduces **HeCLiMP**, a Hebrew minimal-pair benchmark on determiner–noun gender/number agreement (per the search summary of the abstract). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.6/). Relevance: the only recent Hebrew morphological minimal-pair resource found. **(i) (essential)**
- **CLAMS** (Mueller et al., 2020) is said to include Hebrew subject–verb agreement pairs; this was mentioned only in a search summary and not opened. **Verify before citing.**
- **Arabic agreement-based adversarial attacks**: an earlier black-box attack breaking Arabic noun–adjective agreement reduced sentiment accuracy by ~53%, per a search summary; the primary source was not opened. A 2026 robustness study: Anwar Alajmi, Ayed Salman, Imtiaz Ahmad. "Evaluation of Adversarial Robustness in Arabic Language Models." arXiv:2607.25814, Jul 2026. arXiv-only. Evaluates five Arabic LMs under character-, word-, and sentence-level attacks; the search summary says conjunction manipulation degrades accuracy by up to 58%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25814). Relevance: connective manipulation in a Semitic language parallels MorphEvalHeb's connective swaps. **(i)** (optional)
- **NeoDictaBERT** (the paper's backbone) is on arXiv as 2510.20386 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20386). Presumably already cited; listed only for completeness.

### Inferences
- Positioning: MultiBLiMP/TurBLiMP/HeCLiMP perturb morphology to test *grammatical knowledge*. MorphEvalHeb perturbs morphology to create *source-inconsistent but often still grammatical* summaries, e.g., binyan swaps (active → passive/causative) that change who did what to whom. That makes it a semantic, not syntactic, minimal-pair resource.

### Gaps
- I found no morphological-perturbation benchmark for factual consistency in Arabic, Turkish, Finnish, or Hebrew. I did not search Finnish specifically.

## Q5 (scope item 4). Claim decomposition methods used for building training data

### Takeaway
The standard atomic-claim decomposition references are FActScore (atomic facts), WiCE (claim splitting for entailment), Dense X / propositions, Wanner et al. 2024 (decomposition quality matters; DecompScore), and Molecular Facts (atomic is too context-poor, so decontextualize minimally). LLM-Oasis is the key example of decomposition used **to build training negatives**. MorphEvalHeb should cite Wanner et al. and Molecular Facts to justify its decomposition granularity and recomposition step.

### Cited Findings
- **FActScore**: Sewon Min, Kalpesh Krishna, Xinxi Lyu, Mike Lewis, Wen-tau Yih, Pang Wei Koh, Mohit Iyyer, Luke Zettlemoyer, Hannaneh Hajishirzi. "FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation." EMNLP 2023, pp. 12076–12100. PR. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.741/). **(i)**
- **WiCE**: Ryo Kamoi, Tanya Goyal, Juan Diego Rodriguez, Greg Durrett. "WiCE: Real-World Entailment for Claims in Wikipedia." EMNLP 2023, pp. 7561–7583. PR. Splits claims into sub-claims for fine-grained entailment. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.470/). **(i)**
- **A Closer Look at Claim Decomposition**: Miriam Wanner, Seth Ebner, Zhengping Jiang, Mark Dredze, Benjamin Van Durme. *SEM 2024, pp. 153–175. PR. Shows that FActScore-style metrics are sensitive to the decomposition method. Proposes DecompScore and a decomposition prompt based on Russellian logical atomism and neo-Davidsonian semantics. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.starsem-1.13/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11903). **(i) (essential: justifies the decomposition design)**
- **Molecular Facts**: Anisha Gunjal, Greg Durrett. "Molecular Facts: Desiderata for Decontextualization in LLM Fact Verification." Findings of EMNLP 2024, pp. 3751–3768. PR. Argues that fully atomic facts lose context and defines decontextuality and minimality criteria. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.20079). **(i)**
- **Dense X Retrieval (propositions)**: Tong Chen, Hongwei Wang, Sihao Chen, Wenhao Yu, Kaixin Ma, Xinran Zhao, Hongming Zhang, Dong Yu. "Dense X Retrieval: What Retrieval Granularity Should We Use?" arXiv:2312.06648 (I believe it was published at EMNLP 2024, but the arXiv record lists no journal_ref, so verify). Defines propositions as atomic, self-contained factoids. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06648). **(i)** (minor)
- **LLM-Oasis**: claim extraction → falsify one claim → regenerate (see Q1). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.cl-1.1/). **(i)**
- **FactCG**: uses graph/subgraph decomposition instead of textual atomic claims to build multi-hop training data (see Q1). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.258/). **(i)**

### Inferences
- Decomposition in a morphologically rich language raises a problem the English work does not discuss. Hebrew clitics, construct-state chains, and pro-drop mean that splitting into atomic claims can itself introduce agreement or reference errors. The paper should report decomposition/recomposition fidelity, e.g., the rate at which an unperturbed decompose→recompose output stays entailed by the source, in the spirit of Wanner et al.'s DecompScore.

### Gaps
- I found no claim-decomposition study for Hebrew or another Semitic language.
- I did not verify a venue for Dense X Retrieval.
