# Representation probing, activation engineering on encoders, and RL with faithfulness rewards: literature notes and research leads

Compiled 2026-11-21 for the MorphEvalHeb ARR Jan 2027 submission. I opened every arXiv or Anthology page cited here unless the entry is marked **(snippet)**, meaning I saw it only in a search result. Papers already known to us (ActAdd, CAA, RepE, ITI, SAPLMA, INSIDE, CCS, 2505.24859, Posterior-GRPO 2508.05170) are not re-reviewed. None of the papers below supersedes them for our setting.

## (a) Papers by area

### Area 1: Probing and representation analysis (factuality detectors and MRL morphology)

| Paper | Venue / year | URL | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orgad, Toker, Gekhman, Reichart, Szpektor, Kotek, Belinkov. *LLMs Know More Than They Show: On the Intrinsic Representation of LLM Hallucinations* | arXiv 2024 (v4 2025) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02707 | Truthfulness signal in decoder hidden states is concentrated at specific tokens. Probes predict which *error type* the model will make, but they do not transfer across tasks, so there are several truthfulness encodings rather than one. Decoder generator, not an evaluator. |
| Xu, Su, Yu, Xu, Choi, Zhou, Liu. *Identifying Factual Inconsistencies in Summaries: Grounding LLM Inference via Task Taxonomy* | EMNLP 2024 Findings | https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12821 | An error-type taxonomy (predicate, entity, circumstantial, coreference, addition) injected into LLM inference gives SOTA zero-shot detection on 10 datasets, and it can be distilled into small models. The taxonomy is used behaviourally, not probed. |
| Chan, Zeng, Ji. *Interpretable Automatic Fine-grained Inconsistency Detection in Text Summarization* (FineGrainFact) | ACL 2023 Findings | https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.402 (seen via search; arXiv 2305.14548) **(snippet)** | Multi-label prediction of 4 error types (intrinsic/extrinsic × NP/predicate) using SRL frames and doc-fact attention. This is the closest prior work on *error-type* prediction, and it is English-only and not a probing study. |
| Shmidman, Shmidman, Koppel. *NeoDictaBERT: Pushing the Frontier of BERT models for Hebrew* | arXiv 2025 (2510.20386) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20386 **(snippet)** | NeoBERT-architecture Hebrew encoders (monolingual and bilingual) that are SOTA on most Hebrew benchmarks. It contains no layer-wise analysis. |
| Shmidman et al. *DictaBERT* | arXiv 2023 (2308.16687) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16687 **(snippet)** | Fine-tuned morphology heads (gender, number, person, tense) are SOTA. It reports no per-layer probing. |
| Seker et al. *AlephBERT* | ACL 2022 | https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.4 **(snippet)** | Recovers morphological segments from final-layer embeddings. It reports no per-layer analysis. |
| Acs, Hamerlik, Schwartz, Smith, Kornai. *Morphosyntactic probing of multilingual BERT models* | Nat. Lang. Eng. 2024 (arXiv 2306.06205) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06205 **(snippet)** | 247 probes over 42 languages (mostly Indo-European), studying where in the *context* the disambiguating information sits. Hebrew is marginal. |
| Shapiro, Paullada, Steinert-Threlkeld. *A multilabel approach to morphosyntactic probing* | EMNLP 2021 Findings (arXiv 2104.08464) | **(snippet)** | mBERT probes that include Hebrew (and zero-shot Arabic) show gender, case and similar features are linearly extractable. The study is not layer-wise. |
| *Is Probing All You Need? Indicator Tasks…* (arXiv 2310.15905) | 2023 | **(snippet)** | Probes AlephBERTGimmel with UD morphological features, Hebrew among 3 languages. It is the only Hebrew-encoder probing work found. |
| Nadipalli. *Layer-Wise Evolution of Representations in Fine-Tuned Transformers: Insights from SAEs* | arXiv 2025 (2502.16722) | **(snippet)** | SAEs on BERT layers 3/6/12 before and after fine-tuning. Early layers stay stable and late layers specialise. The task is not NLI. |
| Alakeel, Qwaider, Aldarmaki, Alqahtani. *Morphemes Without Borders: Evaluating Root-Pattern Morphology in Arabic Tokenizers and LLMs* | LREC 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15773 | Tokenizer alignment with gold morphological segmentation is neither necessary nor sufficient for producing correct root-pattern forms across 7 LLMs. This is relevant to our root-pattern error type. |

### Area 2: Activation engineering and causal analysis of encoders, metrics and judges

| Paper | Venue / year | URL | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Belrose et al. *LEACE: Perfect linear concept erasure in closed form* | NeurIPS 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819 **(snippet)** | Closed-form linear erasure, plus "concept scrubbing" applied layer by layer. Used to measure how much LMs depend on POS. |
| *Analyzing the Inner Workings of Transformers in Compositional Generalization* (arXiv 2502.15277) | 2025 | **(snippet)** | LEACE scrubbing of syntactic concepts. Probe accuracy drops to 0 and only small task drops follow. The authors caution that LEACE is linear only. |
| AACL 2026 short paper 21 (title not retrieved) | AACL 2026 | https://aclanthology.org/2026.aacl-short.21.pdf **(snippet)** | LEACE brings a gender probe to chance but moves only about 0.4% of occupation-logit energy. Erasure sensitivity is not the same as causal mediation, so it is a methodological caution for us. |
| Ravfogel/Amini et al. *Naturalistic Causal Probing for Morpho-Syntax* (arXiv 2205.07043) | TACL 2023 | **(snippet)** | Spanish noun gender and number are encoded in a few directions in mBERT, RoBERTa and GPT-2. |
| Rozanova et al. *Estimating the Causal Effects of Natural Logic Features…* (arXiv 2404.02622 / 2305.08572) | 2023–24 | **(snippet)** | Interventional (behavioural) causal-effect estimation on NLI models for monotonicity and lexical relations. It does not intervene on activations. |
| Roytburg, Bozoukov, Nguyen, Barzdukas, Fu, Oozeer. *Breaking the Mirror: Activation-Based Mitigation of Self-Preference in LLM Evaluators* | NeurIPS 2025 workshops | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03647 | CAA and optimised steering vectors cut judge self-preference by up to 97%, beating prompting and DPO. They are unstable on legitimate preferences, which suggests the bias is not purely linear. |
| Xu, Li, Liu, Wang, Li, Song, Chen. *Inside the Unfair Judge: A Mechanistic Interpretability Account of LLM-as-Judge Bias* | arXiv Jul 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11871 | Across 7 judges and 7 bias types, biased inputs shift along low-dimensional *type-specific* subspaces. Steering along them induces or reverses bias, and a linear projection predicts judge failure on unseen benchmarks. **This is the closest analogue to "error-type directions" for a decoder judge.** |
| Ramprasad, Wallace. *Do Automatic Factuality Metrics Measure Factuality? A Critical Evaluation* | arXiv 2024 (v4 Nov 2025; NeurIPS 2025 per snippet) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16638 | An MLP on shallow surface features rivals SOTA factuality metrics. SummaC, AlignScore and UniEval are oversensitive to benign edits, and most metrics can be inflated by appending innocuous sentences. The evidence is behavioural, with no internal analysis. |
| Chen et al. *AdvEval: Unveiling the Achilles' Heel of NLG Evaluators* | ACL 2024 Findings | https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14646 | A black-box LLM-driven adversarial generator causes large drops across 12 evaluators. It uses no RL and has no morphology focus. |

Searches for "activation patching on a BERT-style NLI cross-encoder" and "concept erasure applied to a factuality metric" found **no direct prior work**.

### Area 3: RL and preference optimisation with faithfulness rewards, and RL-trained judges and attackers

| Paper | Venue / year | URL | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roit et al. *Factually Consistent Summarization via RL with Textual Entailment Feedback* (RLEF) | ACL 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00186 | An NLI entailment reward improves faithfulness, and the authors explicitly warn about degenerate and extractive collapse, which they counter with KL to an anchor. It cannot fix errors the NLI model misses. **This is the canonical reward-hacking precedent for NLI rewards.** |
| Feng et al. *Improving Factual Consistency of News Summarization by Contrastive Preference Optimization* | EMNLP 2024 Findings | https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19347 | Contrastive preference optimisation plus probing-based training to separate faithful from fabricated tendencies in LLM summarisers. |
| Ye, Santos-Rodriguez, Simpson. *Optimising Factual Consistency in Summarisation via Preference Learning from Multiple Imperfect Metrics* | arXiv 2605.26840 (page says EMNLP 2025 Findings) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26840 | Aggregating several weak factuality metrics into DPO pairs, and discarding pairs where the metrics disagree, avoids single-metric reward instability. |
| *CANOE / Dual-GRPO: Teaching LLMs to Maintain Contextual Faithfulness via Synthetic Tasks and RL* (arXiv 2505.16483) | AAAI 2026 | **(snippet)** | Rule-based rewards on synthetic QA transfer to long-form faithfulness, including summarisation. |
| *TrueBrief: Faithful Summarization through Small Language Models* (arXiv 2601.04212) | 2026 | **(snippet)** | DPO on preference data built by *controlled hallucination injection*, which is structurally the same as our injected-error pipeline. English only. |
| Whitehouse, Wang, Yu, Li, Weston, Kulikov, Saha. *J1: Incentivizing Thinking in LLM-as-a-Judge via RL* | ICLR 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10320 **(snippet)** | GRPO-trained judges with verifiable rewards, plus consistency rewards against position bias. |
| Su et al. (Apple). *Learning to Reason for Hallucination Span Detection* (RL4HS) | arXiv 2025 (ICLR 2026 per snippet) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02173 | GRPO with a span-F1 reward and class-aware advantage scaling (CAPO) to fix the bias toward predicting "no hallucination". Beats SFT on RAGTruth. |
| *SOPO: Socratic Guided Policy Optimization for Span-Level Hallucination Detection* (arXiv 2610.32212) | 2026 | **(snippet)** | Follow-up to RL4HS. |
| Min, Pham, Zhang, Sun. *CORVUS: Red-Teaming Hallucination Detectors via Internal Signal Camouflage* | arXiv Jan 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14310 | LoRA-trained adversary (not RL) that hides internal hallucination signals from probe-based and training-free detectors. |
| Garcia-Estrada, Escolano, Fonollosa. *Reference-Free RL Fine-Tuning for MT: A Seq2Seq Perspective* | arXiv May 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15976 | GRPO on NLLB with a LaBSE+COMET-Kiwi reward over 13 languages. It is competitive with SFT on morphologically complex languages and is the only RL paper found that touches morphology typology. |
| *Reward Models are Metrics in a Trench Coat* (arXiv 2510.03231) | 2025 | **(snippet)** | Argues that reward models and evaluation metrics share failure modes. |

### Area 4: Hebrew, Arabic and other MRLs × faithfulness, error taxonomies

| Paper | Venue / year | URL | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paz-Argaman, Mondshine, Achi Mordechai, Tsarfaty. *HeSum* | ACL 2024 Findings / LoResMT | https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03897 **(snippet)** | A Hebrew abstractive summarisation dataset (10k). ROUGE correlates *negatively* with human judgement. It has no faithfulness metric. |
| Shmidman et al. *DictaLM 2.0* (arXiv 2407.07080) | 2024 | **(snippet)** | Hebrew LLM. Summarisation is evaluated on 75 docs with an LLM judge that includes "consistency". |
| HebNLI (HF dataset, HebArabNlpProject) | 2024 | **(snippet)** | MultiNLI machine-translated to Hebrew by Gemini (293k train). A possible extra training or probing resource. |
| Alansari, Luqman. *AraHalluEval* | ArabicNLP 2025 (arXiv 2509.04656) | **(snippet)** | Fine-grained hallucination indicators for Arabic summarisation (named entity, value, **grammar**, fabrication, inference) with manual evaluation. This is the closest MRL error taxonomy, and it is far coarser than our 20 types. |
| Qiu et al. *mFACT: Detecting and Mitigating Hallucinations in Multilingual Summarisation* | EMNLP 2023 | https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.551 **(snippet)** | Faithfulness metric transferred from English. It shows more hallucination in low-resource languages and has no morphology analysis. |
| Kang, Blevins, Zettlemoyer. *Comparing Hallucination Detection Methods for Multilingual Generation* (arXiv 2402.10496) | 2024 | **(snippet)** | NLI metrics work in high-resource languages and degrade in low-resource ones. |
| *MORPHOGEN* (Agarwal et al.) | ACL 2026 | https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.105/ | Gender-aware morphological rewriting benchmark (French/Arabic/Hindi). LLMs are weak at morphological gender. **No Hebrew.** |
| IMPACT inflectional-morphology benchmark (incl. Hebrew) | 2025 | **(snippet, no arXiv ID found)** | LLMs struggle with rare patterns and with judging ungrammatical inflections. |

## (b) Gaps: no work found on these

1. **No layer-wise probing of a Hebrew encoder** (AlephBERT, DictaBERT, NeoDictaBERT) for gender, number, tense, construct state or roots. The DictaBERT and AlephBERT papers report only final-layer and fine-tuned-head results. *Construct state (smichut) has never been probed* in any model.
2. **No probing of a fine-tuned factuality/NLI cross-encoder for *where* individual error types become separable.** Error-type probing exists only for decoder *generators* (Orgad et al. 2024) and decoder *judges* (Inside the Unfair Judge, 2026).
3. **No concept erasure (LEACE/INLP/RLACE) or activation patching applied to a faithfulness metric** to test whether the metric *uses* a linguistic feature. Ramprasad & Wallace show shallow-feature reliance only behaviourally.
4. **No steering or erasure work on an encoder classifier used as an evaluator.** All evaluator-steering work (Breaking the Mirror, Unfair Judge) is on decoder LLM judges.
5. **No RL or preference optimisation for summarisation faithfulness in Hebrew or any Semitic language**, and no study of NLI-reward hacking in an MRL. Morphology-specific reward hacking (for example, a policy learning surface forms the classifier is blind to) is unstudied.
6. **No RL-trained *attacker* against a factuality metric.** AdvEval is black-box prompting, and CORVUS is LoRA camouflage against internal-signal detectors, not text-level metrics.
7. **No error-type-conditioned analysis across 20 fine-grained morphological categories anywhere.** The richest MRL taxonomy found (AraHalluEval) has 5 summarisation indicators.

## (c) Candidate research leads

Compute estimates assume a MacBook M-series with MPS, our local fine-tuned NeoDictaBERT (about 250M params; check `config.num_hidden_layers`), and a benchmark of about 10³–10⁴ pairs.

| # | Lead | Closest prior | Novelty | MPS cost | Risk | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Layer-wise error-type probing of the MorphEvalHeb cross-encoder.** Extract [CLS] and mean-pooled states per layer. Train linear probes for (i) faithful/unfaithful, (ii) 20-way error type, and (iii) morphological family. Report the "emergence layer" per error type (for example, whether number swap is separable earlier than construct-state or root-pattern confusion). Compare against the *pre-fine-tuning* NeoDictaBERT checkpoint to see what fine-tuning adds. Add UMAP and a silhouette score per type. | Orgad+ 2024 (decoder generator), Inside the Unfair Judge 2026 (decoder judge) | High (first for an encoder metric, an MRL and 20 types) | Low: 1 forward pass per layer, cache to disk, sklearn probes. Hours. | Low. Control tasks or selectivity are needed to rule out probe memorisation. | **1** |
| L2 | **LEACE erasure of error-type and morphological concepts → causal reliance of the metric.** Fit LEACE on layer *k* for a concept (for example, "summary verb gender ≠ article verb gender" or the binary error-type label), scrub, and measure the per-type drop in balanced accuracy. Also erase *generic* morphological gender or number (probe labels from DictaBERT-morph tags) and test which error types collapse. This shows *which linguistic features the metric relies on*. | LEACE 2023, Amnesic probing, Ramprasad & Wallace 2024 (behavioural only) | High | Low–med: LEACE is closed-form (concept-erasure pip package), plus inference re-runs | Medium. Erasure can be ineffective or non-selective (AACL-2026 caution). Report random-direction controls. | **2** |
| L3 | **Activation patching between clean and corrupted pairs per error type.** For minimal pairs (faithful summary vs. the same summary with one injected error), patch residual states at each layer and token position from clean into corrupted and measure logit recovery. This yields a per-type "where is the error detected" map (summary token vs. article token, layer). | Activation patching in decoders (2603.12423, etc.); none on NLI cross-encoders | High | Medium: O(layers × positions) forward passes per pair. Use about 50 pairs per type and patch per layer (not per token) first. Feasible overnight on MPS. | Medium. Minimal pairs must align in token length; LLM-injected errors sometimes change length. | **3** |
| L4 | **Steering the metric: add or subtract error-type directions to flip verdicts.** Diff-of-means directions per type (CAA-style) added at layer *k* on faithful inputs should create false "unfaithful" verdicts, and subtracting them should hide real errors. This tests linearity and *type-specificity* (a cross-type transfer matrix). It mirrors Unfair Judge for encoders. | Inside the Unfair Judge 2026; Breaking the Mirror 2025; CAA | Med–High | Low | Low–med. Results may simply restate L1. Frame it as the causal confirmation of L1. | **4** |
| L5 | **Robustness via "blind spots": the metric's sensitivity matrix.** Use L2/L3 to find error types the metric under-represents, then show a targeted data-augmentation fix (retrain with more of those types) closes the gap. Pairs the diagnosis with a remedy. | Ramprasad & Wallace 2024; AdvEval 2024 | Medium | Medium: one more fine-tuning run of NeoDictaBERT on MPS (feasible, hours) | Low | **5** |
| L6 | **Small-scale RL / DPO against the MorphEvalHeb reward → morphology-specific reward hacking.** Fine-tune a small Hebrew-capable generator (for example, a ≤1B multilingual model or DictaLM-small with LoRA) with DPO pairs ranked by our classifier, or GRPO with the classifier as reward. Measure whether the policy (a) becomes extractive (RLEF failure mode) or (b) exploits error types the metric is weak on (from L1/L2). | RLEF 2023; Ye+ 2026; TrueBrief 2026; Posterior-GRPO | High (first Hebrew/MRL RL-for-faithfulness) | **High** for GRPO on MPS. DPO+LoRA on a ≤1B model is borderline (days). Realistically needs a cloud GPU. | High: compute, Hebrew generator quality, and scope creep for one paper | **7** |
| L7 | **RL / search-based adversarial attacker against the metric, restricted to morphology edits.** A policy (or cheaper: greedy/beam search over single-morpheme edits from DictaBERT-morph inflection alternatives) finds *meaning-changing* edits the metric still labels faithful. Gives an "attack success rate per error type". | AdvEval 2024 (black-box LLM), CORVUS 2026 | High | Low for search-based (encoder inference only); high for true RL | Medium. You must verify that the edits really change meaning (human or LLM check). | **6** |
| L8 | **Cross-model representation comparison: NeoDictaBERT metric vs. multilingual NLI (mDeBERTa-XNLI) vs. HebNLI-fine-tuned model.** Use CKA plus per-type probe curves to test whether the Hebrew-specific encoder encodes morphological error types earlier or more linearly. Supports the "why a Hebrew encoder" claim. | Acs+ 2024; Kang+ 2024 | Medium | Low–med (3 models × forward passes) | Low | **8** |
| L9 | **SAE on the cross-encoder's best layer → interpretable "error detector features".** Train a small SAE (for example 768→4096) on cached activations and label features by error-type enrichment. | Nadipalli 2025; ClassifSAE 2025 (snippet) | Medium | Medium (SAE training on cached activations is fine on MPS) | Medium. SAE features may not align with types, and the evaluation burden is high. | **9** |

**Suggested package for the ARR paper:** L1 + L2 + L4 (optionally L3) as one "What does a Hebrew faithfulness metric know, and what does it use?" analysis section. Each is cheap on MPS and covers Gaps 1–4. L7 (search-based) adds a robustness angle cheaply. Defer L6 (RL) to future work or a follow-up paper unless GPU access appears.
