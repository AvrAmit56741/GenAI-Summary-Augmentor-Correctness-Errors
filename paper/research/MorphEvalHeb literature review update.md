# MorphEvalHeb novelty holds, but baselines need upgrading

**Novelty is intact.** Four independent search streams, covering the literature up to 2026-11-12, found **no concurrent Hebrew summary-factuality metric, benchmark, or trained verifier**, and no injection pipeline anywhere that uses morphological operators (binyan, construct state, agreement) to create factual-consistency negatives. Every multilingual factuality benchmark that was checked either leaves Hebrew out (Mu-SHROOM, PsiloQA, HalluVerse25, Halluverse-M^3) or has not been confirmed to include it. The one exception is mFAVA (EMNLP 2025). It includes Hebrew only as a silver set, gives Hebrew the **highest estimated hallucination rate of 30 languages (16.81%)**, and its detector recovers only **~33% recall** on Hebrew. That is a ready-made sentence for your motivation. The weak point is the baseline suite rather than novelty. Since May 2026, a reviewer can reasonably expect four things: an open Hebrew-native LLM judge (Dicta-LM 3.0, Feb 2026), an open multilingual judge (M-Prometheus), a current small synthetic-data verifier (FactCG, which supersedes MiniCheck on LLM-AggreFact), and a multilingual encoder detector (LettuceDetect v2 on mmBERT, mid-2026). The review also surfaced three critiques you should answer in the paper. TrueTeacher argues that perturbed negatives do not match real model errors. Stress Testing (ACL 2026) shows that metrics penalize meaning-preserving rewrites. A growing set of 2025–2026 papers shows LLM judges are unreliable outside English. Your Kendall-τ results (vanilla judge 0.60, G-EVAL 0.24) are the first Hebrew data point in that judge-reliability literature, so present them that way. Finally, **HeSum's first author is Tzuf Paz-Argaman** (arXiv and ACL Anthology), so change the bib key `mondshine-etal-2024-hesum` to match.

## No concurrent Hebrew work, and every multilingual benchmark skips Hebrew

The closest Hebrew-adjacent work in the window does not compete with yours. **Dicta-LM 3.0** (Shmidman et al., arXiv 2602.02104, Feb 2026) includes a Hebrew chat benchmark with a 1,000-document summarization task. It is scored by **GPT-4o pairwise win rate against Gemini-2.5-Pro reference summaries**, with no factuality decomposition and no morphological error analysis ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02104)). The strongest Hebrew lab therefore evaluates summarization with exactly the unvalidated, English-centric judge that MorphEvalHeb argues against. **mFAVA** (ul Islam, Lauscher, Glavaš, EMNLP 2025) is the only factuality study found that includes Hebrew. It shows multilingual detectors miss most Hebrew hallucinations: P = 82.75, R = 32.97, F1 = 44.19 on the Hebrew silver set ([arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12769v4)). It covers long-form QA, not summarization. The nearest methodological neighbour in Hebrew is **Siani, Kernerman & Kernerman (arXiv 2603.11743, Mar 2026)**. They inject controlled **gender and number agreement errors** into English→Hebrew MT output to train BERT/XLM-R quality-estimation models ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11743)). It is MT QE rather than summary factuality, so cite it as prior Hebrew morpho-syntactic injection, not as a competitor.

Outside Hebrew, the closest analogues come from other morphologically rich languages (MRLs), and none provides a trained, morphology-aware verifier. **AraHalluEval** (Arabic, 12 fine-grained indicators for summarization and generative QA) is a human-annotation framework ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04656)). **HalluVerse25** and **Halluverse-M^3** (Arabic, Turkish, Hindi) build fine-grained data by controlled injection. They find detection degrades outside English and that summarization is harder than QA ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06920)). **BASSE** (Basque/Spanish) is the closest meta-evaluation analogue. It finds **consistency is the hardest criterion**, with mean absolute correlation with humans of 0.201 (Basque) and 0.257 (Spanish), and open judges perform poorly ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17039)). Morphology-aware minimal-pair resources (MultiBLiMP, TurBLiMP, RuBLiMP, HeCLiMP) perturb morphology to probe *grammaticality*, not *factual consistency*. HeCLiMP covers only Hebrew determiner–noun agreement, and MultiBLiMP only subject–verb agreement ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.6/); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.tacl-1.10/)). A safe novelty claim is: *"to our knowledge, the first Hebrew-native factual-consistency framework, and the first injection pipeline whose operators target inflectional and derivational morphology."* Keep the "to our knowledge" hedge. Late-2026 proceedings (EMNLP 2026 main) were not exhaustively indexed.

The English-only status of the strongest checkers also helps your motivation. FactCG, Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B, Granite Guardian 3.3, HHEM-2.1-open and Lynx all carry an `en` language tag on their Hugging Face cards. MiniCheck's paper states it was "trained exclusively on English data" ([MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10774v2)). On LLM-AggreFact, small specialized checkers match or beat frontier judges: **Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B 77.4 and FactCG-DeBERTa-L (0.4B) 75.6, against Claude-3.5 Sonnet 77.2 and GPT-4o 75.9** ([leaderboard](https://llm-aggrefact.github.io/)). Do not cite the leaderboard's unattributed top entries (nimble-v4-max, Clef, Decider) as specific models. Note too that LLM-AggreFact reports balanced accuracy, not Kendall τ.

## Ten baselines ranked by reviewer value and Hebrew feasibility

Your current comparison set covers lexical, embedding and NLI metrics, an English-trained detector (SummaC), a multilingual metric (mFACT), and LLM judges (G-EVAL, vanilla judge). It has three gaps: no open Hebrew LLM, no post-2024 trained verifier, and no multilingual encoder detector. The table orders additions by how likely a reviewer is to ask for them, weighed against cost. Every new external model is zero-shot on Hebrew, so frame them as *cross-lingual-transfer* baselines. Where the backbone tokenizes Hebrew poorly, run each both natively and translate-to-English. That pairing doubles as the translate-test ablation reviewers will want, and Thellmann et al. (2026) give you a citation for why translation is a confound ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24904)).

Lower-priority options are **HHEM-2.1-open** (flan-t5 tokenizer, so MT only), **Lynx** (CC-BY-NC, English), and an intermediate-NLI variant of your own classifier fine-tuned first on **HebNLI** or **LCHAIM**. That last one is an ablation, not a competitor. For the **multi-label detector** there is no released 2025–2026 summarization-specific typed detector in any non-English language. The feasible comparisons are therefore FineSurE-style prompting (row 5), a **FineGrainFact**-style multi-label head reimplemented on NeoDictaBERT, and collapsing your 24 families onto Tang et al.'s four types (intrinsic/extrinsic × NP/predicate) so you can report against that literature. HAD and FAVA checkpoints are English/Chinese and would need translation, so treat them as optional. Rows 1–5 plus the translate-test pairing are the minimum set that makes the Kendall-τ table look current.

## Seven objections reviewers will now raise, and the evidence for answers

**"Synthetic perturbations don't look like real errors."** This is the most predictable objection, and it is well sourced. TrueTeacher states that perturbing human summaries "fails to match real model outputs and misses many error types" ([Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.127/)). Goyal & Durrett (2021) showed poor transfer from synthetic to real errors on XSum, and AMRFact says prior perturbations lack coherence or error-type coverage ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09521)). Your human-labelled Kendall-τ set already partly answers this if it consists of *naturally generated* LLM summaries. Say so explicitly, report τ separately on natural versus injected items if both exist, and add baseline #9 if budget allows.

**"Your paraphrase positives could be gamed, or your metric could be brittle."** Stress Testing (ACL 2026) shows reference-free metrics, AlignScore and SummaC-ZS in particular, give inconsistent scores to meaning-preserving rewrites ([Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1472/)). This supports your back-translation positives. It also invites a cheap robustness table: apply its seven perturbation types (or Hebrew-adapted ones) to faithful summaries and show MorphEvalHeb stays stable while SummaC and BERTScore move.

**"The vanilla LLM judge is a straw man."** Fu & Liu (κ ≈ 0.3 across languages), MM-Eval, Mehrabi et al. (judge identity outweighs the prompt), and Rating Roulette (low intra-rater reliability) together say one judge with one seed is weak evidence either way. Doğruöz et al. (EMNLP-F 2026) explicitly criticize single-judge studies ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02235)). Answer with several judges (GPT-4o-mini, Dicta-LM 3.0, M-Prometheus), variance over seeds, and a FaithJudge-style few-shot judge. Then cite BASSE's 0.20–0.26 consistency correlations as context: your 0.60 judge is not an artificially weak competitor.

**"How do 24 families relate to standard taxonomies?"** Prior taxonomies have 4–11 types (Tang et al. 4, FAVA 6, DiaSumFact 6, LongNovel 8, HAD 11), and none has a morphological category. Provide a mapping table from your 24 families to FRANK (semantic-frame / discourse / content-verifiability) and to Tang et al.'s four types. The mapping also enables a collapsed-label comparison. Because 24 is unusually fine, report per-family support and inter-annotator agreement, and use DiaSumFact's ~0.25 macro-F1 as a reference for how hard multi-label typing is.

**"Are agreement errors factual errors at all?"** Your taxonomy needs to separate two kinds of edit. Some morphological edits change meaning: a binyan swap that changes agent or voice, or construct-state attachment. Others only break grammaticality: agreement violations, which is what HeCLiMP and MultiBLiMP test. Without that line, a reviewer can argue the detector learns fluency, not faithfulness.

**"Decomposition and recomposition in Hebrew introduce their own errors."** Clitics, construct chains and pro-drop make atomic splitting lossy, and no Hebrew decomposition study exists. Report recomposition fidelity, i.e., how often an unperturbed decompose→recompose output is still judged entailed, in the spirit of Wanner et al.'s DecompScore ([Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.starsem-1.13/)). Cite Decomposition Dilemmas and ElementCheck to defend scoring whole summaries at inference.

**"Why not just translate to English and use MiniCheck or FactCG?"** Run that ablation (the MT columns of baselines #3, #4 and #6). Pair it with Thellmann et al. (translation errors measurably distort evaluation), STEMF and M-Prometheus (native beats translated data), and mFAVA (Hebrew recall ~33%). Translation can erase or invent gender, number and definiteness distinctions, so an English pivot is structurally blind to part of your taxonomy. That is currently an inference. The ablation would turn it into evidence.

## Conclusion

Over the past six months the field moved toward MorphEvalHeb's thesis without reaching it. Multilingual detectors (LettuceDetect v2, PsiloQA, STEMF) and judge-reliability studies (Fu & Liu, Doğruöz et al., Mehrabi et al.) now say native, encoder-based, human-validated evaluation is needed outside English, and none of them covers Hebrew. The paper therefore no longer has to argue the premise. It can cite it, and spend its space on what is new: morphology as a *semantic* perturbation axis, and the first Hebrew judge-reliability numbers.

The risk has shifted from "is this novel?" to "is the comparison current?" A paper that beats ROUGE, BERTScore, SummaC and G-EVAL but not Dicta-LM 3.0, M-Prometheus or FactCG will read as dated in a 2026–27 review cycle. Conversely, a strong result against a Hebrew-native 24B judge would be the paper's most persuasive number. Spend the next experimental budget there, and on a natural-error evaluation slice that answers TrueTeacher.
