# How fresh are the generators that new evaluation metrics/benchmarks are tested on?
## Scope: long-form factuality, RAG/grounded QA, LLM-as-judge / reward models, dialogue & data-to-text

Compiled 2026-11-18 for the Hebrew factual-consistency metric paper (ACL).

**Verification legend.** (v) = checked in the paper itself (arXiv HTML / ar5iv / project README) during this survey. (mem) = from memory, not re-checked. (unverified) = could not confirm; treat as uncertain. Release dates of generator models are (mem) unless stated: they are public-knowledge dates, approximate to the month.

**Two roles are kept separate:**
- **Generators**: the models whose outputs the metric/judge is tested on.
- **Metric/judge LLM**: the LLM inside the metric, or the judge being benchmarked.

**Age gap** = paper year (first arXiv version; venue year in brackets) minus the release year of the newest generator. A gap of 0 means the newest evaluated outputs come from the same year the paper appeared.

## Patterns per task family

### Long-form factuality (FActScore → SAFE → VeriScore → FactBench → HalluLens → VeriFastScore)
- **Typical age gap: 0.** Every paper in this family evaluates the newest models available at submission, often within 1–2 months of their release (GPT-4 in FActScore, Claude 3 Opus in SAFE, GPT-4o in VeriScore, o3-mini/Claude 3.7 in VeriFastScore). The exception is HalluLens, which uses about 4-month-old closed models and skips o1/Claude 3.5.
- **Frontier/closed inclusion:** nearly universal. GPT-4-class closed models are always present; SAFE is closed-only.
- **Sizes:** usually a ladder from about 2B to the largest open model (65B → 132B → 405B) plus closed models. The size spread is used to show that the metric separates models.
- **Refresh practice:** emerging. FactBench (annual prompts, regenerated responses) and HalluLens (dynamic test generation) build refresh into the benchmark design.
- **Caveat that matters for us:** human validation often lags the generator set. SAFE validates against human labels on 2022–23 FActScore outputs and then scores 2024 frontier models. VeriScore notes that F1@K depends on generator output length.

### RAG / grounded QA
- **Typical age gap: 0–1 year.** RAGAS (ChatGPT only), ARES (mostly synthetic mock systems; real GPT-4 only in a side experiment) and RAGTruth (GPT-4-0613 plus Llama-2) are 2023 snapshots. The corpora are frozen, so they age with each new model release. LLM-AggreFact deliberately mixes 2019–2023 generators and was updated after release.
- **Frontier/closed:** usually one GPT-3.5/GPT-4 tier plus 7–70B open models. FACTS Grounding is the exception: closed frontier only (incl. o1) with a maintained leaderboard.
- **Evidence on generator dependence:** the strongest evidence here concerns hallucination rate by generator (RAGTruth: GPT-4 lowest). FaithEval finds that "larger models do not necessarily exhibit improved faithfulness". ARES shows that judges fail to transfer across languages. ContextualJudgeBench shows that even o1 reaches only about 55% consistent accuracy as a grounded-output judge.

### LLM-as-judge / reward models
- **Typical age gap: 0–1 year at arXiv, up to 2 years at the venue.** JudgeLM (ICLR 2025, generators from early 2023) and RewardBench (Findings 2025, completions from 2023) are the stale cases. Prometheus 2 (May 2024) is still validated on mid-2023 13B/GPT-3.5/Bard responses.
- **Frontier/closed:** often missing among the generators, even when frontier models are used as judges or teachers (GPT-4 is the teacher for Prometheus/JudgeLM/TIGERScore while the evaluated responses come from 7–13B open models). JudgeBench and FACTS Grounding are the frontier-generator exceptions.
- **Saturation/refresh is explicit in this family.** RewardBench: "Some subsets are solved by small RMs, reaching 100% accuracy". RewardBench 2 reports a 20-point drop and uses unseen prompts to avoid contamination. JudgeBench: "As LLMs become more advanced … requiring stronger judges to evaluate them." MT-Bench: GPT-4 agrees with humans better "when significant performance differences exist between the models".
- **Direct degradation evidence:** Shelf-Life (2025) shows that judges trained on older-generator outputs lose accuracy on newer/stronger generators. JudgeBench shows lower accuracy on stronger-generator pairs. No Free Labels shows that judges succeed mainly on items they could solve themselves. Dorner et al. bound the benefit of a judge that is not stronger than the evaluated model.

### Dialogue / data-to-text / multi-task metrics
- **Typical age gap: 2–4 years for pre-2024 metrics.** UniEval (2022) and GPTScore (2023/NAACL 2024) are meta-evaluated on SummEval/QAGS/FED/SFRES/BAGEL outputs from 2015–2020 systems (RNNs, BART, PEGASUS, Meena). No GPT-3/ChatGPT outputs appear among the generators, even in GPTScore, which uses GPT-3 as the metric. TIGERScore reuses the same old meta-eval sets plus a few 2023 instruction-following outputs.
- **Shift since 2024:** Kasner & Dušek (ACL 2024) create fresh inputs to avoid contamination and evaluate contemporary open 7B models plus GPT-3.5. Even so, the generators are small or mid-tier rather than frontier (GPT-4 serves as the metric).
- **Implication:** in these legacy task families, an age gap of several years was the accepted norm for metric papers until about 2023. The field has since moved toward same-year generators.

### Cross-family takeaways for our paper
1. **Norm since 2023:** a new factuality/grounding metric is expected to be tested on outputs from current models, including at least one closed frontier model, plus an open-model size ladder. A gap of 0–1 year is typical. Gaps of 2 years or more appear mainly in pre-LLM-era summarization/dialogue/D2T metrics (UniEval, GPTScore) and in venue-delayed judge papers (JudgeLM, RewardBench).
2. **Reviewers will know the saturation/staleness literature** (RewardBench → RewardBench 2, JudgeBench, Shelf-Life, FactBench/HalluLens refresh). A paper that validates a metric only on older generators should say so explicitly and ideally add a fresh-generator slice.
3. **Validation lag is common and accepted if acknowledged.** SAFE validates on 2022–23 human labels and then applies to 2024 models. Reporting how the metric behaves on a newer-generator subset (even a small one) follows precedent.
4. **Judge/metric vs. generator strength:** several 2024–25 papers show that judge accuracy drops when generators are stronger or closer in quality (MT-Bench, JudgeBench, Shelf-Life, No Free Labels, Dorner et al.). Self-preference (FACTS: +3.23%) argues against using a same-family LLM as both generator and judge.
